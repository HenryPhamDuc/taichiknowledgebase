24-Form Tai Chi: Step-by-Step Analysis (Forms 17-24)¶

Overview¶

This final section covers forms 17-24 of the 24-Form Tai Chi, including the most challenging movements: low postures, advanced balances, complex sequences, and the closing form.

Prerequisites: - Comfortable with Forms 1-16 - Able to squat low (Snake Creeps Down) - Single-leg balance proficiency (30+ seconds) - Fluid transitions between movements

Forms Covered: 17. Snake Creeps Down (She Shen Xia Shi 蛇身下势) 18. Golden Rooster Stands on One Leg (Jin Ji Du Li 金鸡独立) - 2 repetitions 19. Parting Wild Horse's Mane (Ye Ma Fen Zong 野马分鬃) - reprise 20. Fair Lady Works at Shuttles (Yu Nu Chuan Suo 玉女穿梭) - 4 corners 21. Needle at Sea Bottom (Hai Di Zhen 海底针) 22. Fan Through the Back (Shan Tong Bei 闪通背) 23. Turn, Deflect, Parry, Kick (Zhuan Shen Ban Lan Chui 转身搬拦捶) 24. Closing Form (Ru Feng Si Bi 如封似闭)

Training Emphasis for This Section: - Extreme low postures (Forms 17, 21) - Advanced single-leg balances (Form 18) - Multi-directional movement (Form 20) - Complex sequencing (Form 23) - Return to stillness (Form 24)

Form 17: Snake Creeps Down (She Shen Xia Shi 蛇身下势)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Snake lowering body while maintaining alertness and readiness

Key Training Focus: - Lowest posture in entire 24-Form - Single-leg squat strength - Hip flexibility and openness - Maintaining structure in deep position

Martial Context: Evading high attack, attacking opponent's lower body

Difficulty Level: Advanced - requires significant leg strength and flexibility

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Transition from Strike Opponent's Ears¶

Starting Position: End of Strike Opponent's Ears (double fist)

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Both fists open, palms face down
3. Right foot pivots on heel (toes turn out 45 degrees)
4. Left foot prepares to step
5. Weight remains on left leg (preparation)

Structure:
- Hands: Lower to waist level, palms down
- Eyes: Look in direction of movement (left-front)
- Posture: Upright, preparing to descend

Phase 2: Step and Descend¶

Movement:
1. Step left foot forward (heel first)
2. Weight shifts to left leg
3. Right leg extends straight (almost locked)
4. Body descends into deep squat
5. Right hand sweeps down past right leg
6. Left hand rises to protect head

Final Position:
- Left leg: Bent deeply (knee over toes, not beyond)
- Right leg: Extended straight, inside edge of foot on ground
- Left hand: Protects head, palm faces right
- Right hand: Sweeps past right knee, palm faces down
- Hips: Low (as close to ground as flexibility allows)
- Eyes: Look forward (not down)

Key Points:
- Descend with control (don't drop)
- Keep spine straight (don't hunch)
- Right leg straight but not hyperextended
- Hips sink between legs (not behind)
- Speed: 6-8 seconds for descent

Common Mistakes:
❌ Left knee collapses inward
   → Correction: Knee tracks over second toe

❌ Right leg bends (not straight)
   → Correction: Actively straighten right knee

❌ Back rounds (hunches forward)
   → Correction: Crown lifts, tailbone drops, spine elongates

❌ Too high (not low enough)
   → Correction: Lower as far as flexibility allows without compromising structure

❌ Looking down
   → Correction: Eyes level, forward gaze

Testing:
- In lowest position, partner gently pushes your shoulder
- Correct: Stable, can rise smoothly
- Incorrect: Collapses or stuck in position

Phase 3: Hold and Express¶

Duration: Hold for 2-3 seconds

Internal Quality:
- Like snake ready to strike
- Low but alert
- Compressed power (spring coiled)
- Breath deep into abdomen (not shallow chest)

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Ground connection through both legs
- Spine elongates despite low position
- Ready to spring upward or strike

Breathing Pattern¶

Preparation:             Natural inhalation
Descent:                 Exhalation (sinking)
Hold:                    Brief pause (empty or natural)
Rise:                    Inhalation (ascending)

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent attacks with high strike

Application: 

1. Descend: Evade incoming high attack
2. Low Position: Under opponent's guard
3. Right Hand: Sweep opponent's leg or grab ankle
4. Left Hand: Protect head from follow-up
5. Options: 
   - Sweep opponent's leg
   - Strike opponent's knee/groin
   - Rise into counter-attack

Strategic Quality:** Unexpected - most expect resistance at same level, not descent

Combat Insight: "Snake Creeps Down - when opponent attacks high, go low. Like water flowing under obstacle. Unexpected angle creates opportunity."

Flexibility and Strength Development¶

If You Can't Go Low Yet:

Exercise 1: Supported Squat¶

1. Hold onto stable surface (wall, chair)
2. Practice deep squat position
3. Gradually reduce hand support
4. Goal: Full squat without support
5. Hold: 30 seconds, 3 repetitions

Exercise 2: Horse Stance Progression¶

1. Start with high horse stance
2. Gradually lower over weeks
3. Hold each level for 30-60 seconds
4. Progress: Lower 1-2cm per week
5. Goal: Thighs parallel to ground

Exercise 3: Hip Opener Stretches¶

Butterfly Stretch:
- Sit, soles of feet together
- Gently press knees toward ground
- Hold: 1-2 minutes
- Frequency: Daily

Pigeon Pose:
- Yoga pigeon pose
- Opens hip rotators
- Hold: 1 minute each side
- Frequency: Daily

Rising from Snake Creeps Down¶

Often Neglected But Critical:

Movement:
1. Inhale as you begin to rise
2. Left leg powers the ascent
3. Right leg assists (doesn't lead)
4. Crown of head lifts first
5. Body rises as one unit
6. Return to standing posture

Key Points:
- Rise with control (don't pop up)
- Spine stays straight throughout
- Breathing coordinates with movement
- Equal attention to rising and descending

Common Error:** Rising too fast (loses benefit, risks injury)

Form 18: Golden Rooster Stands on One Leg (Jin Ji Du Li 金鸡独立) - 2 Repetitions¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Rooster's characteristic one-legged stance

Key Training Focus: - Advanced single-leg balance - Knee lift (can become kick) - Hand coordination (one up, one down) - Vertical alignment in challenging position

Repetitions in 24-Form: Two times (right, left)

Martial Quality: Balanced between yielding and attacking - knee can strike, hands can deflect

Right Golden Rooster¶

Phase 1: Rise from Snake¶

Starting Position: End of Snake Creeps Down

Movement:
1. Rise to standing (as described above)
2. Shift weight 100% to left leg
3. Right knee begins to lift
4. Right hand rises along right side
5. Left hand presses down

Structure:
- Rising from Snake: Smooth, controlled
- Weight transfer: Gradual to left leg
- Eyes: Look forward

Phase 2: Stand on One Leg¶

Movement:
1. Right knee lifts to waist height (or higher)
2. Right foot hangs naturally (ankle relaxed)
3. Right hand rises to head height, palm faces left
4. Left hand presses to left hip, palm down
5. Weight 100% on left leg

Final Position:
- Right knee: Lifted to waist height (or comfortable maximum)
- Right foot: Relaxed, ankle loose
- Right hand: Protects head, palm faces left
- Left hand: Guards hip, palm faces down
- Left leg: Stable, micro-bend (not locked)
- Eyes: Look forward (not at lifted knee)

Key Points:
- Balance comes from alignment, not muscle
- Crown lifts, tailbone drops (axial extension)
- Hands have opposing intention (up/down)
- Standing leg: Engaged but not rigid
- Speed: 4-6 seconds to settle

Common Mistakes:
❌ Standing leg locked (hyperextended)
   → Correction: Maintain micro-bend in knee

❌ Looking down at knee
   → Correction: Eyes forward, level gaze

❌ Hips tilt (one side drops)
   → Correction: Keep hips level, engage core

❌ Right shoulder rises
   → Correction: Drop right shoulder, elongate neck

❌ Gripping with standing foot
   → Correction: Foot relaxed, weight distributed evenly

Testing:
- Hold position for 10-30 seconds
- Partner gently pushes your shoulder (various directions)
- Correct: Stable, minimal movement
- Incorrect: Wobbly, requires arm windmilling

Phase 3: Express and Settle¶

Duration: Hold for 2-3 seconds (or longer in practice)

Internal Quality:
- Calm alertness
- Like rooster surveying surroundings
- Ready to kick or step in any direction
- Breath natural, deep

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Lightness in lifted leg
- Rootedness in standing leg
- Whole-body integration
- Mental calm despite physical challenge

Left Golden Rooster¶

Transition from Right: 

1. Lower right foot slowly (controlled)
2. Shift weight 100% to right leg
3. Left knee lifts
4. Left hand rises, right hand presses
5. Mirror the right-side movement

Key Points: - Lowering should be as controlled as lifting - No "dropping" the foot - Transition is part of the form, not just the hold

Breathing Pattern¶

Lift knee and hands:     Inhalation
Hold:                    Natural breath (don't hold breath)
Lower:                   Exhalation

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Close-range confrontation

Application: 

1. Knee Lift: Can strike opponent's groin or solar plexus
2. Upper Hand: Protects head from strikes
3. Lower Hand: Guards body, can strike ribs
4. Single Leg: Ready to kick or step immediately
5. Balance: Can absorb contact without falling

Strategic Quality:** Compact power - small movement, significant effect

Combat Principle: "Golden Rooster - seemingly passive, actually ready to strike with knee, hands, or standing leg kick. Opponent underestimates at their peril."

Balance Progression Training¶

Beginner to Advanced:

Level 1: Wall Support¶

1. Stand beside wall, fingertips touching
2. Lift knee to comfortable height
3. Use wall only for micro-adjustments
4. Hold: 10 seconds each leg
5. Progress: Reduce finger pressure

Level 2: No Support, Eyes Open¶

1. Stand without wall
2. Lift knee to waist height
3. Hold: 20-30 seconds each leg
4. Focus: Alignment over muscle
5. Quality: Calm, not tense

Level 3: Eyes Closed¶

1. Stand on one leg
2. Close eyes
3. Hold: 10-20 seconds
4. Develops: Proprioception, not visual dependency
5. Progress: Increase to 1 minute

Level 4: Dynamic Challenge¶

1. From Golden Rooster position
2. Slowly lower lifted knee halfway
3. Raise back up
4. Repeat: 5-10 times each leg
5. Develops: Dynamic balance strength

Form 19: Parting Wild Horse's Mane (Ye Ma Fen Zong 野马分鬃) - Reprise¶

Movement Overview¶

Context: This form appeared earlier (Form 2), but now performed with deeper understanding

Key Differences (Reprise vs. Original): - Deeper stance (more leg strength developed) - Clearer waist rotation (better integration) - More refined peng jin (quality of expansion) - Smoother transitions (more practice)

Training Approach: - Not "already learned - move on" - Opportunity to refine and deepen - Apply lessons from Forms 17-18

Execution Notes¶

Apply Everything Learned: 

From Snake Creeps Down: Hip openness, low center of gravity
From Golden Rooster: Single-leg stability, vertical alignment
From Earlier Forms: Waist rotation, weight transfer, peng jin

Quality Should Be Noticeably Different Than First Repetition:
- More rooted
- More expansive
- More integrated
- More intentional

Common Refinements at This Stage¶

Aspect Early Practice Now (After Forms 17-18)
Stance height Higher, safer Lower, stronger
Waist rotation Arm-driven Dantian-initiated
Weight transfer Unclear at times Absolutely definitive
Peng quality Conceptual Felt, tangible
Balance Occasional wobble Stable, calm
Intent Learning sequence Martial application clear

Training Insight: This is why traditional forms repeat movements - not filler, but opportunity for deepening.

Form 20: Fair Lady Works at Shuttles (Yu Nu Chuan Suo 玉女穿梭) - 4 Corners¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Woman weaving at loom - hands move like shuttle passing back and forth

Also Known As: "Jade Girl Works Shuttles"

Key Training Focus: - Multi-directional movement (four corners) - Diagonal stepping patterns - Upward push while advancing - Continuous flow through direction changes

Repetitions in 24-Form: Four times (diagonally: forward-right, forward-left, forward-right, forward-left)

Martial Quality: Advancing strike from multiple angles

Corner 1: Forward-Right¶

Phase 1: Preparation¶

Starting Position: End of Parting Wild Horse's Mane

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right foot steps to the right (not forward)
3. Both hands lower to waist level
4. Hands cross in front of body (right over left)
5. Weight remains on left leg

Structure:
- Right hand: Over left, palms face down
- Arms: Rounded, crossed at wrists
- Eyes: Look in direction of intended movement (diagonal forward-right)

Phase 2: Step and Push¶

Movement:
1. Step right foot diagonally forward-right
2. Weight shifts to right leg (70%)
3. Hands separate: Right hand rises, left hand lowers
4. Right arm pushes diagonally upward
5. Left hand presses to left hip
6. Waist rotates to right

Final Position:
- Right arm: Extended diagonally up-right, palm faces left (inward)
- Left hand: Presses down beside left hip, palm down
- Right leg: 70% weight, bent
- Left leg: 30% weight, straight
- Waist: Faces diagonal forward-right
- Eyes: Look over right hand

Key Points:
- Diagonal direction (45 degrees from center)
- Right arm pushes upward (not straight forward)
- Waist rotation coordinates with hand separation
- Speed: 6-8 seconds
- Quality: Upward, expansive

Phase 3: Settle¶

Duration: Hold for 1-2 seconds

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Upward intention (like shuttle rising through warp threads)
- Rooted despite diagonal expansion
- Ready to change direction for next corner

Corner 2: Forward-Left¶

Transition:¶

1. Shift weight 100% to right leg
2. Left foot pivots on heel (faces forward-left)
3. Hands circle: Left rises, right lowers
4. Step left foot diagonally forward-left
5. Mirror the first corner movement

Corners 3 and 4¶

Pattern: 

Corner 1: Forward-right
Corner 2: Forward-left
Corner 3: Forward-right (return toward center)
Corner 4: Forward-left (return toward center)

Total: Four corners of an imaginary rectangle

Spatial Awareness: - Imagine four corners of room - You're moving diagonally through space - Maintain orientation despite direction changes

Common Mistakes¶

Error Effect Correction
Moving straight forward (not diagonal) Loses weaving quality, predictable Consciously step at 45-degree angle
Hands push straight up (no forward) Loses martial intention Push diagonally (up AND forward)
Feet turn in wrong direction Loses power, awkward Feet point in direction of movement
Rushing direction changes Loses flow, stumbles Pause briefly between corners
Looking down at feet Loses awareness, poor balance Eyes level, look in direction of travel

Breathing Pattern¶

Preparation (hands cross):  Natural inhalation
Step and push:              Exhalation
Settle:                     Brief pause
Transition to next corner:  Natural inhalation

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Multiple opponents or need to change angles

Application: 

1. Diagonal Step: Evade direct attack line
2. Upward Push: Deflect opponent's guard, strike chin/neck
3. Lower Hand: Control opponent's structure
4. Direction Changes: Confuse opponent, create angles
5. Continuous Flow: Don't give opponent time to reset

Strategic Quality: Unpredictable angles, continuous pressure

Combat Insight: "Fair Lady Works Shuttles - like weaving, passing through opponent's defense from unexpected angles. Four corners, four opportunities."

Form 21: Needle at Sea Bottom (Hai Di Zhen 海底针)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Needle sinking to ocean floor - deep, focused, precise

Key Training Focus: - Extreme forward bend (lowest posture after Snake) - Single-leg weight support - Downward intention (opposite of most Tai Chi movements) - Maintaining structure in deep position

Martial Context: Attacking opponent's lower body, grabbing ankle

Difficulty Level: Advanced - requires hip flexibility and hamstring length

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Prepare¶

Starting Position: End of Fair Lady Works at Shuttles (Corner 4)

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right foot steps forward, toe touches
3. Right hand circles down
4. Left hand rises to protect head
5. Weight remains on left leg (preparation)

Phase 2: Descend¶

Movement:
1. Inhale (preparation)
2. Exhale as you descend
3. Right hand reaches down toward ground
4. Left hand rises to protect head
5. Body bends forward from hips (not waist)
6. Weight 100% on left leg

Final Position:
- Right hand: Reaches toward ground (as low as possible)
- Left hand: Protects head, palm faces right
- Right leg: Empty, toe may touch for balance
- Left leg: 100% weight, bent (squat depth varies)
- Torso: Bent forward (45-90 degrees, depending on flexibility)
- Eyes: Look down (only form where this is appropriate)

Key Points:
- Bend from hips, not waist (protect lower back)
- Spine stays long (doesn't round excessively)
- Right hand intention: Reaching deep
- Left hand: Protective, counterbalancing
- Depth: As low as flexibility allows without compromising structure

Common Mistakes:
❌ Bending from waist (not hips)
   → Correction: Hinge at hip joint, keep spine long

❌ Rounding upper back excessively
   → Correction: Chest slightly out, back long

❌ Left shoulder rises
   → Correction: Drop left shoulder, elongate neck

❌ Right leg bears weight
   → Correction: Keep 100% on left leg

❌ Looking forward (not down)
   → Correction: This form - looking down is correct

Testing:
- In lowest position, can you rise smoothly?
- Correct: Controlled ascent
- Incorrect: Struggles or uses momentum

Phase 3: Express Deep Intention¶

Duration: Hold for 1-2 seconds

Internal Quality:
- Like needle sinking through water
- Focused, precise intention
- Not collapsed - still peng despite deep position
- Breath deep, calm

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Reaching deep connection
- Humility (literally lowering yourself)
- Ready to spring back up

Breathing Pattern¶

Preparation:             Inhalation
Descent:                 Complete exhalation
Hold:                    Brief pause (empty lungs)
Rise:                    Inhalation

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent standing, you attack low

Application: 

1. Descend: Under opponent's guard
2. Right Hand: Grab opponent's ankle or strike knee
3. Left Hand: Protect head from counter-attack
4. Intention: Disrupt opponent's root
5. Options:
   - Yank ankle (opponent falls)
   - Strike knee joint
   - Sweep leg while rising

Strategic Quality:** Unexpected attack angle - most defend middle/high, not ground level

Combat Principle: "Needle at Sea Bottom - opponent looks up, you strike down. Like needle finds bottom regardless of water depth, your intention finds opponent's root."

Flexibility Considerations¶

If You Can't Reach Ground:

DO NOT:
- Force yourself down (risks injury)
- Round back excessively
- Bend knee of standing leg too much

DO:
- Reach as low as YOUR current flexibility allows
- Focus on hinging from hips
- Keep spine long
- Progress will come with practice (weeks/months)

Accept your current range while gently expanding it

Rising from Needle¶

Movement:
1. Inhale as you begin to rise
2. Left leg powers ascent
3. Crown of head lifts first
4. Spine unrolls vertebra by vertebra
5. Right hand rises naturally
6. Return to standing

Key Points:
- Rise with control (don't pop up)
- No dizziness (rising too fast)
- Spine stays long throughout

Form 22: Fan Through the Back (Shan Tong Bei 闪通背)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Force penetrating through opponent's entire back

Also Known As: "Penetrating Punch Through the Back"

Key Training Focus: - Powerful forward strike - Whole-body integration - Waist rotation generating force - Vertical alignment despite forward intention

Martial Quality: Devastating single strike - can end confrontation

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Rise and Prepare¶

Starting Position: End of Needle at Sea Bottom

Movement:
1. Rise to standing (as described)
2. Shift weight 100% to left leg
3. Right foot steps forward
4. Right hand circles back (preparing to strike)
5. Left hand rises to protect head

Structure:
- Right hand: Circles back beside right ear (likeBeginning Form but reversed)
- Left hand: Protects head, palm faces right
- Weight: 100% on left leg (preparation)
- Eyes: Look forward (target direction)

Phase 2: Strike¶

Movement:
1. Step right foot forward
2. Weight shifts to right leg (70%)
3. Right hand strikes forward (fist or palm)
4. Left hand presses down to left hip
5. Waist rotates to right
6. Right foot follows (heel lifts)

Final Position:
- Right hand: Strikes forward, shoulder height, palm or fist forward
- Left hand: Presses down beside left hip, palm down
- Right leg: 70% weight, bent
- Left leg: 30% weight, straight
- Waist: Faces forward
- Eyes: Look over striking hand

Key Points:
- Strike from whole body, not just arm
- Power originates from rear leg drive
- Waist rotation adds torque
- Weight shift coordinates with strike
- Speed: 3-4 seconds (quicker than most forms)

Common Mistakes:
❌ Using only arm strength
   → Correction: Feel power from leg drive

❌ Shoulders rise during strike
   → Correction: Exhale fully, drop shoulders

❌ Leaning forward
   → Correction: Spine vertical, power from structure

❌ Weight 50/50
   → Correction: Clear 70/30 distribution

Testing:
- At completion, feel connection from striking hand to ground
- Correct: Continuous power line
- Incorrect: Disconnected, arm only

Phase 3: Settle and Root¶

Duration: Hold for 1-2 seconds

Internal Quality:
- Power already delivered (not ongoing tension)
- Rooted, stable
- Ready to continue or conclude
- Breath complete exhalation

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Satisfying completion of powerful movement
- Not aggressive - calm power
- Like thunder that has already struck

Breathing Pattern¶

Preparation:             Natural inhalation
Strike:                  Sharp exhalation (can vocalize "ha")
Settle:                  Brief pause (empty)

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent vulnerable, decisive strike needed

Application: 

1. Opponent off-balance or exposed
2. Step in aggressively
3. Strike: Solar plexus, chin, or center of chest
4. Whole body mass behind strike
5. Intention: Penetrate THROUGH target (not surface impact)

Target Areas:
- Solar plexus (breath disruption)
- Chin (stuns, can render unconscious)
- Center of chest (sternum, disrupts balance)

Power Quality: Not surface hit - penetrating force through entire body

Combat Warning: "Fan Through the Back - potentially devastating strike. Practice with supreme control. In application, use only when absolutely necessary."

Form 23: Turn, Deflect, Parry, Kick (Zhuan Shen Ban Lan Chui 转身搬拦捶)¶

Movement Overview¶

Most Complex Sequence in 24-Form

Named After: Four distinct actions in rapid sequence: 1. Turn (zhuan shen) 2. Deflect/Move (ban) 3. Parry/Block (lan) 4. Punch/Strike (chui)

Key Training Focus: - Multi-part coordination - Direction change (180-degree turn) - Sequential defense leading to offense - Timing and rhythm

Martial Quality: Complete defensive sequence culminating in strike

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Turn (Zhuan Shen)¶

Starting Position: End of Fan Through the Back

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to right leg
2. Pivot on right heel (180-degree turn to rear)
3. Left foot circles around to face new direction
4. Both hands lower to waist level
5. Weight remains on right leg (completion of turn)

Key Points:
- Turn is smooth, not jerky
- Head turns with body (not whipping around)
- Maintain balance throughout rotation
- Eyes: Look in new direction

Duration: 2-3 seconds

Phase 2: Deflect/Move (Ban)¶

Movement:
1. Left hand circles up and outward
2. Right hand circles down and back
3. Weight shifts slightly to left leg (but mostly right)
4. Deflecting motion (imaginary opponent's arm)

Structure:
- Left hand: Deflects outward, palm faces right
- Right hand: Prepares at hip, palm faces up
- Weight: Mostly right (can move if needed)
- Eyes: Look forward (new direction)

Martial Intent:** Redirect opponent's incoming strike

Duration: 2-3 seconds

Phase 3: Parry/Block (Lan)¶

Movement:
1. Right hand rises to protect centerline
2. Left hand lowers to guard
3. Weight stabilizes (70% left, 30% right)
4. Protective posture

Structure:
- Right hand: Guards center, palm faces left
- Left hand: Guards lower, palm faces down
- Weight: Mostly left (preparation for kick)
- Eyes: Forward, alert

Martial Intent:** Block or parry follow-up attack

Duration: 2-3 seconds

Phase 4: Kick (Chui - actually a punch, despite "kick" in name)¶

Wait - Correction: This form ends with a PUNCH, not kick despite name

Movement:
1. Weight shifts to left leg
2. Right foot steps forward
3. Right hand forms fist and punches forward
4. Left hand pulls to left hip
5. Weight shifts to right leg (70%)

Final Position:
- Right fist: Punches forward, shoulder height, knuckles forward
- Left hand: Pulls to left hip, palm down
- Right leg: 70% weight, bent
- Left leg: 30% weight, straight
- Eyes: Look over right fist

Key Points:
- Punch from whole body, not arm
- Sequential flow: Turn → Deflect → Parry → Punch
- Each action clear but connected
- Speed: 8-12 seconds for entire sequence

Common Mistakes:
❌ Rushing the sequence
   → Correction: Each action clear, then connect

❌ Losing balance during turn
   → Correction: Practice turn separately first

❌ Confusing order of actions
   → Correction: Memorize sequence: Turn, Deflect, Parry, Punch

❌ Punch uses only arm
   → Correction: Power from legs, waist, then arm

Breathing Pattern¶

Turn:                    Natural breath
Deflect:                 Inhalation
Parry:                   Brief pause
Punch:                   Sharp exhalation

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Multiple opponents or complex attack sequence

Application: 

1. Turn: Face new threat or reorient
2. Deflect: Redirect first incoming attack
3. Parry: Block follow-up attack
4. Punch: Counter-attack when opponent vulnerable

Strategic Quality:** Complete defensive-offensive sequence

Combat Principle: "Turn, Deflect, Parry, Punch - opponent attacks, you redirect twice, then strike. Never meet force with force - redirect, then counter."

Form 24: Closing Form (Ru Feng Si Bi 如封似闭)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: "Like closing a shutter" or "as if sealing and closing"

Also Called: "Apparent Close Up"

Key Training Focus: - Return to beginning (full circle) - Completion and integration - Gathering energy - Transition from movement to stillness

Martial Context: Concluding engagement, creating distance, returning to neutral

Philosophical Quality: End is beginning - Closing Form contains seed of Beginning Form

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Separate and Draw Back¶

Starting Position: End of Turn, Deflect, Parry, Punch

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right fist opens, palm faces down
3. Both hands separate (shoulder-width apart)
4. Palms face down
5. Weight remains on left leg (preparation)

Structure:
- Hands: Shoulder-width, palms down
- Arms: Rounded, not straight
- Eyes: Forward
- Posture: Upright

Phase 2: Draw Back (Withdrawal)¶

Movement:
1. Inhale as hands draw back toward body
2. Hands circle inward (not straight back)
3. Weight shifts back to left leg (100%)
4. Right foot becomes empty (toe may lift)
5. Hands draw to chest level

Key Points:
- Circular withdrawal (not linear)
- Inhalation coordinates with draw
- Weight completely to back leg
- Quality: Gathering, collecting

Martial Intent:** Creating distance, gathering for final push

Phase 3: Press Forward (Final Push)¶

Movement:
1. Exhale as hands press forward
2. Hands press down and out (final position)
3. Right foot settles (if it lifted)
4. Weight: 50/50 distribution
5. Return to Beginning Form position

Final Position: Beginning Form¶

Structure:
- Feet: Parallel, shoulder-width apart
- Weight: Evenly distributed (50/50)
- Knees: Soft, not locked
- Hips: Level, pelvis neutral
- Spine: Naturally straight
- Crown: Lifts toward sky
- Shoulders: Relaxed, arms hang naturally
- Hands: Rest lightly against thighs
- Eyes: Soft, gaze forward
- Breath: Natural, deep into abdomen

Mental State:
- Mind quiet, focused on present
- Practice complete but not ended
-Integration of all 24 forms
- Stillness contains all movement

Duration: Stand quietly for 30-60 seconds (or longer)

Breathing Pattern¶

Separate and draw back:   Complete inhalation
Press forward:            Complete exhalation
Final stillness:          Natural breathing (or no-breath awareness)
