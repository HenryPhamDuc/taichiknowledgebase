24-Form Tai Chi: Step-by-Step Analysis (Forms 9-16)¶

Overview¶

This intermediate section covers forms 9-16 of the 24-Form Tai Chi, introducing more complex movements including single-leg balances, low postures, and multi-directional stepping patterns.

Prerequisites: - Comfortable with Forms 1-8 - Able to stand on one leg for 10+ seconds - Basic understanding of weight transfer and waist rotation - Daily practice habit established

Forms Covered: 9. Playing the Pipa (Shou Hui Pi Pa 手挥琵琶) 10. Retreat and Repulse Monkey (Dao Nian Hou 倒撵猴) - 4 repetitions 11. Left Diagonal Flying (Xie Fei Shi 斜飞势) 12. Wave Hands Like Clouds (Yun Shou 云手) - 3 repetitions 13. Single Whip (Dan Bian 单鞭) 14. High Pat on Horse (Gao Tan Ma 高探马) 15. Heel Kick (Right) (You Deng Jiao 右蹬脚) 16. Strike Opponent's Ears (Shuang Feng Guan Er 双峰贯耳)

Training Emphasis for This Section: - Balance challenges (Forms 9, 14, 15) - Backward stepping (Form 10) - Large, expansive movements (Forms 11, 12, 13) - Transitioning between high and low postures

Form 9: Playing the Pipa (Shou Hui Pi Pa 手挥琵琶)¶

Cultural Context¶

Named After: Traditional Chinese stringed instrument (pipa/lute)

Visual Quality: Hands positioned as if holding and playing pipa - left hand higher (on neck), right hand lower (on body)

Key Training Focus: - Compact structure (close to body) - Upward intention - Weight distribution (40/60, not extreme 70/30) - Ready posture (transitional, but complete in itself)

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Step Forward (Preparation)¶

Starting Position: End of third Brush Knee and Push (left side)

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right foot steps forward, heel touches
3. Right hand circles down and back
4. Left hand rises slightly
5. Weight remains on left leg (100%)

Structure:
- Right hand: Circles back to right side of body
- Left hand: Rises to chest height
- Eyes: Look forward
- Posture: Upright, ready to receive

Phase 2: Settle Into Posture¶

Movement:
1. Right heel lowers to ground
2. Weight shifts forward slightly (40% right, 60% left)
3. Left hand rises to nose height, palm faces right
4. Right hand lowers to chest level, palm faces left
5. Both elbows dropped, arms rounded
6. Distance between hands: About 30cm (12 inches)

Final Position:
- Left hand: Height of nose, palm faces right, fingers up
- Right hand: Height of sternum, palm faces left, fingers up
- Arms: Rounded as if holding pipa
- Weight: 40% right leg, 60% left leg
- Stance: Short (feet closer than shoulder-width)
- Eyes: Look through left hand
- Body: Upright, crown lifts

Key Points:
- Compact posture (hands close to body centerline)
- Upward intention (playing instrument)
- Weight mostly back (ready to yield or advance)
- Elbows sink (don't fly out to sides)
- Speed: 4-6 seconds to settle

Common Mistakes:
❌ Arms too wide (elbows out)
   → Correction: Imagine holding large ball against chest

❌ Weight 50/50
   → Correction: Feel slightly more weight back (60/40)

❌ Left hand too high (above head)
   → Correction: Hand height = nose level

❌ Leaning back
   → Correction: Spine vertical, not tilted backward

Testing:
- Partner pushes your left hand backward
- Correct: Structure holds, rooted in both legs
- Incorrect: Upper body collapses backward

Phase 3: Intent and Stillness¶

Duration: Hold for 2-3 seconds

Internal Quality:
- Listen (ting jin) - sensitivity to opponent
- Ready to respond in any direction
- Calm alertness
- Upward energy (like music rising from instrument)

Breathing Pattern¶

Step forward:          Natural inhalation
Settle into posture:   Exhalation
Hold:                  Brief pause (empty or natural breath)

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent throws punch

Application: 

1. Step In: Close distance
2. Left Hand Rises: Deflect opponent's arm upward
3. Right Hand: Control opponent's elbow or wrist
4. Posture: Ready to step in further or yield back
5. Intention: Disrupt, don't resist

Quality: Not aggressive - receptive and responsive, like musician listening while playing

Classical Principle: "Playing the Pipa - listen with hands, respond with whole body. Opponent's force becomes your music."

Form 10: Retreat and Repulse Monkey (Dao Nian Hou 倒撵猴) - 4 Repetitions¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Monkey retreating while defending - stepping back while maintaining forward intention

Unique Quality: Only form in 24-Form that moves backward continuously

Key Training Focus: - Backward stepping (unusual in Tai Chi) - Maintaining structure while retreating - Hand coordination (one pushes, one pulls) - Weight transfer in reverse direction

Repetitions in 24-Form: Four times (right, left, right, left)

Martial Strategy: Yield while countering - strategic retreat, not fleeing

First Repetition (Right Side)¶

Phase 1: Turn and Prepare¶

Starting Position: End of Playing the Pipa

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right foot pivots on heel (toes point outward 45 degrees)
3. Left foot prepares to step back
4. Right hand rises to right ear level
5. Left hand lowers to hip level

Preparation Structure:
- Right hand: Palm faces forward, beside right ear
- Left hand: Rests on left hip, palm down
- Weight: 100% on left leg
- Eyes: Look in direction of retreat (backward)

Key Points:
- Turn prepares for backward movement
- Right hand guards head
- Left hand ready to push
- Weight fully on front leg (can step back freely)

Phase 2: Step Back and Push¶

Movement:
1. Step left foot backward (toe lands first)
2. Weight shifts to left leg (now rear leg)
3. Right hand pushes forward from ear
4. Left hand pulls back to left hip
5. Right foot pivots to face forward
6. Weight distribution: 70% left (rear), 30% right (front)

Final Position:
- Right hand: Pushes forward, palm out, shoulder height
- Left hand: Presses down beside left hip, palm down
- Left leg: 70% weight, bent
- Right leg: 30% weight, straight
- Waist: Faces forward
- Eyes: Look over right hand

Key Points:
- Step back lightly (toe first, then entire foot)
- Push and pull happen simultaneously
- Maintain forward intention despite backward movement
- Speed: 4-6 seconds per repetition
- Quality: Yielding but countering

Common Mistakes:
❌ Stepping back heavily (stomping)
   → Correction: Place toe first, roll foot down gently

❌ Leaning back (falling into retreat)
   → Correction: Keep spine vertical, retreat with control

❌ Both hands push (no pull)
   → Correction: Right pushes, left pulls (opposing forces)

❌ Looking down
   → Correction: Eyes level, look over pushing hand

Testing:
- At completion, partner pushes your right hand
- Correct: Rooted in rear leg, stable
- Incorrect: Top-heavy, falls backward easily

Phase 3: Settle and Root¶

Movement:
1. Complete exhalation
2. Feel ground connection through rear (left) leg
3. Maintain peng in right arm
4. Left hand continues pressing down
5. Hold for 1 second, prepare next repetition

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Retreat with dignity (not fleeing)
- Counter-attack while yielding
- Calm in face of aggression

Second, Third, Fourth Repetitions¶

Pattern: Alternating sides (right, left, right, left)

Left Side Mirror Movement: 

1. Step right foot back
2. Left hand pushes forward
3. Right hand pulls to right hip
4. Weight 70% on right leg (rear)

Flow: - After first repetition (right): Transition directly to left - After second (left): Transition to right - After third (right): Transition to left - After fourth (left): Hold position, prepare for next form

Rhythm: Each repetition should take 4-6 seconds, creating a flowing retreat: 

Right (4-6s) → Left (4-6s) → Right (4-6s) → Left (4-6s)
Total: 16-24 seconds for complete sequence

Breathing Pattern¶

Preparation (turn):     Natural inhalation
Step back and push:     Exhalation
Settle:                 Brief pause
Transition to next:     Natural inhalation

Martial Application¶

Strategic Context: Opponent attacks aggressively, overwhelming forward defense

Application Logic: 

1. Recognize: Opponent's force too strong to meet directly
2. Retreat: Step back to create space and time
3. Yield: Don't resist opponent's momentum
4. Counter: Push while retreating (simultaneous)
5. Result: Opponent over-commits, loses balance forward

Why Retreat Works:
- Opponent expects resistance
- When you yield, opponent's force continues forward
- Your push exploits opponent's forward momentum
- Less effort, greater effect (classic Tai Chi strategy)

Strategic Principle: "Retreat is not defeat. Retreat to advance, yield to overcome. Monkey retreats but bites while withdrawing."

Backward Stepping Technique¶

Critical Details:

Foot Placement¶

Correct:
- Toe touches first
- Entire foot rolls down smoothly
- Foot points forward (not angled out)
- Quiet placement (no sound)

Incorrect:
- Heel strikes first (clunky, telegraphs movement)
- Foot angles outward (unstable)
- Stomps (loss of control, poor peng)

Weight Transfer¶

Correct Sequence:
1. 100% on front leg
2. Step back (toe touches, 100% still front)
3. Roll foot down (weight still front)
4. Shift weight back (gradual transfer)
5. Settle 70% rear, 30% front

Common Error: Shifting weight during step (unstable, telegraphs intention)

Upper Body Coordination¶

Timing:
- Hand push/pull happens AS weight shifts back
- Not before (telegraphs)
- Not after (loses power)
- Simultaneous: Step settles + hands complete

Quality:
- Pushing hand expands (peng)
- Pulling hand sinks (rooting)
- Shoulders stay relaxed

Common Errors Across All Four Repetitions¶

Error Effect Correction
Rushing retreat Loses stability, looks like fleeing Slow down, each step deliberate
Leaning back Falls backward easily Keep spine vertical, crown lifts
Looking down Loses awareness of opponent Eyes level, peripheral vision
Uneven rhythm Rushes some reps, slows others Count internally, maintain even tempo
Hands not coordinating Push without pull (or vice versa) Feel opposing forces, both active

Training Drills¶

Drill 1: Backward Step Isolation¶

Purpose: Master backward stepping without upper body complexity

Execution:
1. Stand in horse stance
2. Practice stepping back with one leg only
3. Focus: Toe-first placement, quiet, controlled
4. Weight stays front until step complete
5. 10 repetitions each leg

Benefit: Isolates footwork challenge

Drill 2: Wall Retreat¶

Purpose: Maintain vertical alignment while retreating

Setup:
1. Stand with back to wall (2-3 inches away)
2. Don't touch wall
3. Perform Repulse Monkey movement
4. If you lean back, you'll hit wall

Feedback: Immediate awareness of leaning tendency

Drill 3: Partner Guidance¶

Purpose: Develop correct push/pull coordination

Setup:
1. Partner stands beside you
2. Partner places one hand on your pushing hand
3. Partner places other hand on your pulling hand
4. Perform movement slowly
5. Partner provides light resistance

Benefit: External feedback on hand coordination

Form 11: Left Diagonal Flying (Xie Fei Shi 斜飞势)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Diagonal expansion, like bird flying upward at angle

Key Training Focus: - Large, expansive movement - Diagonal power (not straight forward/back) - Waist rotation driving arm movement -Transition from compact (Pipa) to open (Diagonal Flying)

Martial Quality: Expansive strike - covers wide arc, hard to block

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Transition from Repulse Monkey¶

Starting Position: End of fourth Repulse Monkey (left side)

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to right leg (front leg from Repulse Monkey)
2. Left foot steps back and to the left
3. Both hands cross in front of chest (left over right)
4. Weight remains on right leg (preparation)

Hand Position:
- Left hand: Crosses over right, palm faces down
- Right hand: Under left, palm faces up
- Arms: Rounded, crossed at wrists
- Eyes: Look in direction of intended movement (diagonal left-front)

Phase 2: Expand and Rise¶

Movement:
1. Inhale as you expand
2. Weight shifts to left leg (70%)
3. Waist rotates to left
4. Left hand rises diagonally upward (above head)
5. Right hand presses down to right hip
6. Right foot follows (heel lifts)

Final Position:
- Left arm: Extended diagonally up-left, palm faces right (inward)
- Right hand: Presses down beside right hip, palm down
- Left leg: 70% weight, bent
- Right leg: 30% weight, straight
- Waist: Faces diagonal left-front
- Eyes: Look over left hand

Key Points:
- Diagonal direction (not straight forward)
- Left hand rises above head level
- Expansion originates from waist rotation
- Speed: 6-8 seconds
- Feeling: Expansive, uplifting

Common Mistakes:
❌ Movement too small (not diagonal enough)
   → Correction: Extend arm fully, waist rotates 45-60 degrees

❌ Right hip rises with left hand
   → Correction: Right hand presses down as counterbalance

❌ Weight 50/50
   → Correction: Clear 70/30 distribution

❌ Eyes look straight forward
   → Correction: Follow left hand with gaze

Testing:
- Partner pushes your left hip sideways
- Correct: Stable diagonal structure
- Incorrect: Collapses or loses balance

Phase 3: Express and Settle¶

Movement:
1. Complete exhalation
2. Feel expansion through left arm
3. Right hand continues pressing down
4. Head lifts, tailbone drops
5. Hold for 1-2 seconds

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Like bird taking flight
- Diagonal power (harder to resist than straight force)
- Expansive but rooted
- Joyful quality (flying is liberating)

Breathing Pattern¶

Preparation (hands cross):   Natural inhalation
Expand and Rise:             Inhalation (expansion)
Settle:                      Exhalation (rooting)

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent attacks from side angle

Application: 

1. Step Back: Create space and angle
2. Arm Expansion: Strike opponent's face/neck with back of left hand
3. Right Hand: Controls opponent's structure or guards low
4. Diagonal Angle: Harder for opponent to defend
5. Waist Power: Generates striking force

Power Quality: Whipping action - waist rotates, arm follows,hand snaps at end

Combat Insight: "Diagonal Flying - attack from angle opponent doesn't expect. Straight defense meets diagonal offense and fails."

Form 12: Wave Hands Like Clouds (Yun Shou 云手) - 3 Repetitions¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Hands wave like clouds drifting across sky

Unique Qualities: - Only form performed entirely sideways (lateral movement) - Continuous, flowing movement (no pauses) - Waist drives arm waves - Three complete repetitions (step-step-step)

Key Training Focus: - Lateral weight transfer - Continuous waist rotation - Arm waves follow waist (not independent) - Smooth stepping (no up-down bobbing)

Repetitions in 24-Form: Three complete cycles (step to left three times)

Starting Position¶

From: End of Diagonal Flying

Transition:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right foot steps sideways (to the right)
3. Feet parallel, shoulder-width apart
4. Both hands lower to waist level
5. Preparation for cloud movement

Cloud Movement Cycle (One Repetition)¶

Phase 1: Right Cloud (Weight Shift Right)¶

Movement:
1. Shift weight to right leg (70%)
2. Waist rotates to right
3. Right hand rises to right side (shoulder height)
4. Left hand lowers to left hip level
5. Both palms face inward (toward body)

Structure:
- Right hand: Shoulder height, palm faces left
- Left hand: Hip level, palm faces right
- Waist: Faces right
- Eyes: Look over right hand

Key Points:
- Waist rotation drives hand movement
- Hands don't rise independently - follow waist
- Weight clearly 70/30
- Quality: Smooth, continuous

Phase 2: Step Left (Weight Transfer)¶

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to right leg
2. Left foot lifts and steps sideways (left)
3. Toe lands first, then entire foot
4. Weight remains on right leg (preparation)
5. Hands maintain positions briefly

Key Points:
- Step is smooth (no bobbing up-down)
- Foot placement: Shoulder-width (not narrow, not wide)
- Hands pause briefly while stepping
- Eyes shift to left hand

Phase 3: Left Cloud (Weight Shift Left)¶

Movement:
1. Shift weight to left leg (70%)
2. Waist rotates to left
3. Left hand rises to left side (shoulder height)
4. Right hand lowers to right hip level
5. Both palms face inward

Structure:
- Left hand: Shoulder height, palm faces right
- Right hand: Hip level, palm faces left
- Waist: Faces left
- Eyes: Look over left hand

This Completes ONE Cloud Repetition

Complete Sequence (Three Repetitions)¶

Repetition 1:
- Right cloud → Step left → Left cloud (now facing left)
- Step right (preparation for second cloud)

Repetition 2:
- Right cloud → Step left → Left cloud (now facing center)
- Step right (preparation for third cloud)

Repetition 3:
- Right cloud → Step left → Left cloud (now facing left)
- Hold in left cloud position (prepare for Single Whip)

Total Movement: Three steps to the left

Rhythm: Each cloud cycle should take 6-8 seconds: 

Right Cloud (2-3s) → Step (1-2s) → Left Cloud (2-3s) → Step (1s)
Per repetition: 6-8 seconds
Three repetitions: 18-24 seconds total

Common Mistakes¶

Error Effect Correction
Hands wave independently Disconnected from waist, loses power Initiate from waist, hands follow
Bobbing up-down while stepping Unstable, telegraphs movement Keep head at same height throughout
Narrow stance Unstable sideways Maintain shoulder-width throughout
Rushing the clouds Loses cloud quality (slow, drifting) Slow down, feel floating sensation
Eyes don't shift Neck tension, poor awareness Eyes follow rising hand naturally

Breathing Pattern¶

Right Cloud:        Inhalation
Step:               Brief breath hold or natural transition
Left Cloud:         Exhalation
Step:               Natural inhalation (preparation for next)

Continuous, never held

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Multiple opponents attack from sides

Application: 

1. Sideways Movement: Evade direct attack line
2. Rising Hand: Deflect incoming strikes
3. Lowering Hand: Guard against low attacks
4. Continuous Flow: No pause for opponent to exploit
5. Stepping: Change angle continuously

Quality: Defensive movement that creates offensive opportunities

Strategic Insight: "Clouds move sideways, not straight. Similarly, avoid direct confrontation, move to advantageous angle, strike from position of strength."

Training Drills¶

Drill 1: Waist Isolation¶

Purpose: Connect arms to waist rotation

Execution:
1. Stand in horse stance, feet planted (no stepping)
2. Hands in cloud position
3. Rotate waist side-to-side
4. Hands follow waist passively
5. 20 rotations each direction

Benefit: Establishes waist-arm connection

Drill 2: Head-Level Maintenance¶

Purpose: Eliminate up-down bobbing

Setup:
1. Perform clouds beside wall or doorway
2. Top of head lightly touches wall/doorframe
3. If you bob, head loses contact or presses too hard

Feedback: Immediate awareness of vertical stability

Drill 3: Single Cloud Repetition¶

Purpose: Perfect one cycle before linking three

Execution:
1. Perform ONE cloud cycle
2. Hold final position, check structure
3. Return to start
4. Repeat 10 times
5. Then link into three continuous

Benefit: Quality over quantity - perfect one before multiplying

Form 13: Single Whip (Dan Bian 单鞭)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Implements single whip or strike from one side

Key Training Focus: - Hook hand formation (unique in 24-Form) - Wide stance with clear weight distribution - Waist rotation driving arm extension - Expansive posture with opposing forces

Position in Form: Acts as transitional "hinge" - appears multiple times throughout 24-Form

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Transition from Clouds¶

Starting Position: End of third Cloud (left cloud position)

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right foot toes turn in (pivot on heel)
3. Both hands cross in front of chest
4. Right hand begins forming hook
5. Weight remains on left leg (preparation)

Phase 2: Form Hook and Expand¶

Movement:
1. Right hand forms hook (fingertips touch, palm faces down)
2. Hook rises to right side (shoulder height)
3. Left hand circles down then pushes left
4. Weight shifts to left leg (70%)
5. Waist rotates to left
6. Right heel turns out naturally

Final Position:
- Right hand: Hook at shoulder height, palm down
- Left hand: Palm pushes left, shoulder height, fingers up
- Left leg: 70% weight, bent
- Right leg: 30% weight, straight
- Waist: Faces left
- Eyes: Look over left hand

Key Points:
- Hook formed with intention, not grip strength
- Left palm pushes from centerline to left
- Stance is wide (wider than shoulder-width)
- Opposing forces: Hook right, palm pushes left
- Speed: 6-8 seconds

Common Mistakes:
❌ Hook too tense (white knuckles)
   → Correction: Fingertips touch lightly, palm relaxed

❌ Stance too narrow
   → Correction: Feet should be 1.5x shoulder-width

❌ Left arm straight (no peng)
   → Correction: Maintain rounded arm structure

❌ Weight 50/50
   → Correction: Clear 70/30 distribution

Testing:
- Partner pushes your left palm
- Correct: Structure rooted, force transmits to ground
- Incorrect: Upper body moves independently

Phase 3: Settle and Expand¶

Movement:
1. Complete exhalation
2. Feel expansion between hands
3. Hook hand extends right, palm pushes left
4. Head lifts, tailbone drops
5. Hold for 1-2 seconds

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Wide expansive energy
- Like bow drawn (tension between hands)
- Calm power
- Ready to release in any direction

Breathing Pattern¶

Transition (hands cross):  Natural inhalation
Form hook and expand:      Exhalation
Settle:                    Brief pause

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Multiple opponents or need for wide defensive coverage

Application: 

1. Hook Hand: Traps or strikes opponent on right
2. Left Palm: Strikes or pushes opponent on left
3. Wide Stance: Stable base against multiple attackers
4. Waist Rotation: Generates power for both sides
5. Quality: Simultaneous defense on both sides

Combat Principle: "Single Whip - one hand hooks, one hand strikes. Opponents on both sides cannot both succeed."

Form 14: High Pat on Horse (Gao Tan Ma 高探马)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Patting horse's neck from tall posture

Key Training Focus: - Compact posture after expansion (Single Whip) - Weight mostly back (ready to yield or advance) - Upward palm intention - Front foot empty (toe touches)

Martial Context: Preparing to mount horse or strike downward opponent

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Step Forward¶

Starting Position: End of Single Whip

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right foot steps forward, heel touches
3. Right hand unhooks and circles down
4. Left hand circles back
5. Weight remains on left leg (preparation)

Phase 2: Pat and Settle¶

Movement:
1. Right heel lowers
2. Weight shifts forward slightly (30% right, 70% left)
3. Right hand rises to nose height, palm faces down
4. Left hand circles back to left hip, palm down
5. Left toe touches (empty)

Final Position:
- Right hand: Palm faces down, height of nose
- Left hand: Presses down beside left hip
- Right leg: 30% weight, bent
- Left leg: 70% weight, straight
- Left foot: Toe touches ground (empty)
- Eyes: Look over right hand

Key Points:
- Right palm faces down (patting motion)
- Weight mostly back (can kick with left foot)
- Compact posture (hands close to body)
- Speed: 4-6 seconds

Common Mistakes:
❌ Weight 50/50
   → Correction: Clearly 70% back, 30% front

❌ Left foot bears weight
   → Correction: Practice lifting left foot to verify emptiness

❌ Right hand too high (above head)
   → Correction: Hand height = nose level

❌ Leaning forward
   → Correction: Spine vertical, crown lifts

Form 15: Heel Kick - Right (You Deng Jiao 右蹬脚)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Kick delivered with heel

Key Training Focus: - Single-leg balance while kicking - Hand coordination (push before kick) - Heel-focused strike (not toe or instep) - Controlled kick (not wild swinging)

Repetitions in 24-Form: Only right heel kick

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Prepare¶

Starting Position: End of High Pat on Horse

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right hand circles down
3. Left hand rises to cross with right (at wrists)
4. Hands cross in front of chest (left over right)
5. Weight 100% on left leg (preparation for kick)

Hand Position:
- Left hand: Over right, palms face down
- Arms: Rounded, crossed at wrists
- Eyes: Look in direction of kick (forward-right)

Phase 2: Push and Prepare Kick¶

Movement:
1. Hands separate and push outward (sides)
2. Palms face outward (away from body)
3. Right knee lifts to waist height
4. Ankle flexed (heel ready to strike)
5. Weight 100% on left leg

Structure:
- Hands: Shoulder height, extended to sides
- Right knee: Lifted to waist level
- Right foot: Ankle flexed, heel forward
- Eyes: Look forward

Phase 3: Execute Kick¶

Movement:
1. Exhale sharply
2. Right leg extends forward (heel strikes)
3. Kick height: Waist to chest level (not head-high)
4. Hands maintain outward push
5. Left leg stable (slight bend)

Final Position:
- Hands: Extended to sides, shoulder height
- Right leg: Extended forward, heel strikes
- Left leg: Supporting leg, stable
- Eyes: Look over right heel

Key Points:
- Heel strikes (not toe or ball of foot)
- Kick controlled (not swinging wild)
- Hands push outward (opposing force)
- Supporting leg stable (doesn't wobble)
- Speed: 3-4 seconds for entire sequence

Common Mistakes:
❌ Kick too high (loses balance)
   → Correction: Kick to waist/chest height, not head

❌ Looking down at kicking foot
   → Correction: Eyes forward, level gaze

❌ Hands collapse
   → Correction: Maintain outward push throughout

❌ Supporting leg wobbles
   → Correction: Engage standing leg, spread toes

Testing:
- After kick, hold extended position for 3 seconds
- Correct: Stable, controlled
- Incorrect: Wobbly, can't hold position

Phase 4: Retract¶

Movement:
1. Bend right knee, retract leg
2. Lower foot slowly to ground
3. Hands lower to sides
4. Prepare for next form

Control: Lowering should be as controlled as kick

Breathing Pattern¶

Preparation (hands cross):  Natural inhalation
Hands push outward:         Inhalation
Kick:                       Sharp exhalation
Hold:                       Brief pause
Retract:                    Natural inhalation

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent at mid-distance

Application: 

1. Hand Push: Distract or deflect opponent's guard
2. Knee Lift: Prepare kick, can become knee strike
3. Heel Kick: Strike opponent's solar plexus, ribs, or knee
4. Hand Position: Maintain balance, ready to strike

Target Areas:
- Knee (low kick)
- Solar plexus (mid-height)
- Ribs (side angle)

Not Recommended: Head (too high, loses balance)

Combat Principle: "Kick with control, not wild swing. Heel is hardest part of foot - use it like spear point."

Balance Development for Kicking¶

If You Struggle with Single-Leg Balance:

Exercise 1: Wall-Assisted Kick¶

1. Stand beside wall, fingertips touching
2. Practice kick motion
3. Use wall only for micro-adjustments
4. Gradually reduce finger pressure
5. Goal: No wall contact needed

Exercise 2: Retention Training¶

1. Execute kick
2. Hold extended position for 3 seconds
3. Retract slowly (3 seconds)
4. Repeat: 5-10 times each leg
5. Progress: Increase hold time to 5-10 seconds

Exercise 3: Eyes-Closed Balance¶

1. Stand on one leg (no kick yet)
2. Close eyes
3. Hold for 10-20 seconds
4. Progress: Increase to 1 minute
5. Then add small kick motion

Form 16: Strike Opponent's Ears (Shuang Feng Guan Er 双峰贯耳)¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Simultaneous strikes to both ears with fists

Also Known As: "Double Peak Pierces Ears" or "Tiger Seizes Prey"

Key Training Focus: - Dual-fist strike (unusual in Tai Chi) - Forward bow stance - Sinking after kick (from Heel Kick) - Simultaneous two-hand strike

Martial Quality: Devastating double strike to vulnerable targets

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Retract from Kick¶

Starting Position: End of Heel Kick (right leg extended)

Movement:
1. Bend right knee, retract leg
2. Lower right foot (heel touches first)
3. Both hands lower from sides
4. Weight shifts to right leg (preparation)
5. Hands relax, preparing to form fists

Phase 2: Form Fists and Strike¶

Movement:
1. Both hands form fists (thumbs on outside)
2. Fists circle from outside to inside
3. Weight shifts forward to right leg (70%)
4. Left foot follows (heel lifts)
5. Fists strike forward at face height

Final Position:
- Fists: Shoulder-width apart, face height, knuckles forward
- Right leg: 70% weight, bent
- Left leg: 30% weight, straight
- Waist: Faces forward
- Eyes: Look between fists

Key Points:
- Fists formed with intention (not death grip)
- Strike height: Opponent's ears/temples
- Width: Shoulder-width (not too narrow, not wide)
- Power: From leg drive and waist rotation
- Speed: 4-6 seconds

Common Mistakes:
❌ Fists too tight (tension in arms)
   → Correction: Grip firmly but not white-knuckled

❌ Strike too narrow (fists touch)
   → Correction: Maintain shoulder-width distance

❌ Leaning forward
   → Correction: Spine vertical, power from legs

❌ Shoulders rise
   → Correction: Exhale fully, drop shoulders

Phase 3: Settle and Root¶

Movement:
1. Complete exhalation
2. Feel grounding through right leg
3. Maintain structure in arms
4. Head lifts, tailbone drops
5. Hold for 1-2 seconds

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Focused power (both fists unified)
- Sinking quality after kick
- Ready to continue flow

Breathing Pattern¶

Retract from kick:        Natural inhalation
Form fists and strike:    Exhalation
Settle:                   Brief pause

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent vulnerable after failed attack

Application: 

1. Opponent attacks: You deflect or evade
2. Opponent exposed: Head/ears vulnerable
3. Both fists strike simultaneously
4. Targets: Both ears, temples, or jaw
5. Effect: Disorienting, potentially fight-ending

Why Both Fists:
- Doubles impact
- Harder to block (covers wide area)
- Psychological effect (overwhelming)

Combat Warning: "Double Ear Strike - highly effective, potentially dangerous. Practice with supreme control. In real application, can cause permanent damage."

Safety Considerations¶

For Practice: - Never strike partner with full force - Air punch or light contact only - Control is paramount - Eyes open, aware of surroundings

For Application: - Use only in genuine self-defense - Understand legal and ethical implications - Stop when opponent is no longer threat - Tai Chi principle: Yield first, strike last

Summary: Forms 9-16 Progress Path¶

These eight forms introduce intermediate challenges:

Playing the Pipa: Compact structure, upward intention Repulse Monkey: Backward stepping, retreat with counter Diagonal Flying: Large diagonal expansion Wave Hands Like Clouds: Continuous lateral flow Single Whip: Hook hand, wide expansive posture High Pat on Horse: Compact preparation, empty front foot Heel Kick: Single-leg balance, controlled kicking Strike Opponent's Ears: Dual-fist strike, sinking power

Next Forms (17-24): - Snake Creeps Down (low posture challenge) - Golden Rooster Stands on One Leg (advanced balance) - Parting Wild Horse's Mane (reprise with deeper understanding) - Fair Lady Works at Shuttles (corner-to-corner movement) - Needle at Sea Bottom (extreme low posture) - Fan Through the Back (powerful strike) - Turn, Deflect, Parry, Kick (complex sequence) - Closing Form (return to beginning)

Training Recommendation: Spend 2-3 weeks on each form before linking. Quality of each movement determines quality of entire form.

Continue to Forms 17-24 for advanced movements including low postures, complex sequences, and the closing form.
