24-Form Tai Chi: Step-by-Step Analysis (Forms 1-8)¶

Overview¶

This detailed breakdown covers the first 8 movements of the 24-Form Tai Chi (Simplified Beijing Form), created in 1956 to make Tai Chi accessible to beginners while preserving essential principles from traditional Yang-style.

What This Guide Provides: - Precise movement breakdowns for each form - Biomechanical analysis and key principles - Common mistakes and corrections - Breathing coordination - Martial applications (where relevant) - Testing methods for self-verification

Forms Covered: 1. Beginning Form (Qi Shi) 2. Wild Horse Parts Mane (Ye Ma Fen Zong) - 3 repetitions 3. White Crane Spreads Wings (Bai He Liang Chi) 4. Left Brush Knee and Push (Lou Xi Ao Bu) - 3 repetitions 5. Playing the Pipa (Shou Hui Pi Pa) 6. Retreat and Repulse Monkey (Dao Nian Hou) - 4 repetitions 7. Left Diagonal Flying (Xie Fei Shi) 8. Wave Hands Like Clouds (Yun Shou) - 3 repetitions

How to Use This Guide: - Study one form per week, not all at once - Practice each form 10-20 repetitions before moving on - Use the "Testing" section to verify your understanding - Return to previous forms as new insights develop

Form 1: Beginning Form (Qi Shi 起势)¶

Movement Breakdown¶

Starting Position (Wu Chi Stance)¶

Structure:
- Feet parallel, shoulder-width apart
- Weight evenly distributed (50/50)
- Knees soft, not locked
- Hips level, pelvis neutral
- Spine naturally straight
- Crown of head lifts toward sky
- Shoulders relaxed, arms hang naturally
- Hands rest lightly against thighs
- Eyes soft, gaze forward
- Breath natural, deep into abdomen

Mental State:
- Mind quiet, focused on present moment
- Release concerns of past and future
- Feel connection to ground through feet
- Body heavy, mind light

Duration: Stand quietly for 30-60 seconds before beginning movement

Phase 1: Arms Rise (Inhalation)¶

Movement:
1. Inhale slowly and deeply
2. Hands rise forward together
3. Palms face down, fingers pointing forward
4. Arms rise to shoulder height
5. Elbows stay slightly bent (not locked)
6. Shoulders remain relaxed and dropped

Key Points:
- Movement initiated by intention, not muscle
- Hands float up as if buoyed by water
- Speed: Very slow, evenly paced
- Range: Hands rise to exactly shoulder level
- Weight: Remains 50/50 throughout

Common Mistakes:
❌ Shoulders rise with arms
   → Correction: Consciously drop shoulders before beginning

❌ Arms move too fast
   → Correction: Take full 4-6 second inhalation

❌ Locking elbows
   → Correction: Maintain soft, rounded arm structure

❌ Leaning forward
   → Correction: Keep spine vertical, crown lifts

Testing:
- Partner gently presses down on your wrists
- Correct: Structure doesn't collapse, force transmits to ground
- Incorrect: Arms drop or shoulders tense up

Phase 2: Hands Press Down (Exhalation)¶

Movement:
1. Exhale slowly and completely
2. Hands press downward together
3. Palms face down, fingers forward
4. Hands lower to hip level
5. Knees bend slightly as hands descend
6. Return to 50/50 weight distribution

Key Points:
- Hands press as if compressing a spring
- Movement originates from dantian (lower abdomen)
- Knees bend in coordination with hand descent
- Speed: Match exhalation (6-8 seconds)
- Feeling: Sinking, grounding, settling

Common Mistakes:
❌ Hands drop without pressing intention
   → Correction: Feel resistance, like pressing water

❌ Knees don't bend
   → Correction: Coordinate knee bend with hand descent

❌ Buttocks stick out
   → Correction: Tuck tailbone slightly, maintain neutral pelvis

❌ Breath held or rushed
   → Correction: let breath lead the movement

Testing:
- At bottom position, partner pushes your shoulders
- Correct: Stable, rooted, can't be easily moved
- Incorrect: Top-heavy, easily pushed backward

Breathing Pattern¶

Inhalation (4-6 seconds):    Arms rise
Brief pause (1-2 seconds):   Transition at top
Exhalation (6-8 seconds):    Hands press down
Brief pause (1-2 seconds):   Return to stillness

Martial Application¶

Surface Appearance: Simple arm raise Hidden Meaning: Developing peng jin (expansive force)

Application: - Rising arms: Deflect upward-coming strike - Pressing down: Control opponent's structure, sink their root - Standing posture: Develop whole-body connection for all subsequent movements

Training Insight: "The beginning contains the seed of everything that follows. If you rush this form, your entire practice will be shallow. Take time to really feel."

Common Questions¶

Q: How high should my hands rise? A: Exactly shoulder height. Not higher (causes shoulder tension), not lower (loses peng quality). Use a mirror to verify.

Q: Should I feel anything specific? A: Yes - tingling or warmth in hands, heaviness in feet, quiet mind. If you feel nothing yet, you're practicing too fast or too tense.

Q: How many times should I practice this form? A: Daily, 10-20 repetitions. Yes, even advanced practitioners still practice Beginning Form. It's foundational, not elementary.

Form 2: Wild Horse Parts Mane (Ye Ma Fen Zong 野马分鬃) - 3 Repetitions¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Wild horses shaking their manes to release tension and invigorate circulation

Key Training Focus: - Weight transfer (100% to one leg) - Waist rotation coordinating with arm movements - Opening and closing (kai he) - Stepping with proper weight distribution

Repetitions in 24-Form: Three times total (right, left, right)

Right Wild Horse Parts Mane¶

Phase 1: Hold Ball (Preparation)¶

Starting Position: From Beginning Form

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to right leg
2. Left foot turns out 45 degrees (heel pivot)
3. Right foot steps forward, heel first
4. Right hand rises to chest height
5. Left hand lowers to hip level
6. Both palms face each other as if holding ball

Structure:
- Right palm: Up, at chest level (as if supporting ball from below)
- Left palm: Down, at hip level (as if pressing ball from above)
- Distance between hands: About 30cm (12 inches)
- Right knee: Bent, tracking over second toe
- Left leg: Straight but not locked

Key Points:
- Weight still 100% on left leg (preparation)
- Waist faces forward (not rotated yet)
- Eyes look at right hand
- Hold ball for 1-2 seconds, feel structure

Common Mistakes:
❌ Weight already on right leg
   → Correction: Keep weight back until stepping phase

❌ Hands too close or too far apart
   → Correction: Maintain ball-holding distance

❌ Looking down at hands
   → Correction: Eyes level, gaze at right hand

Testing:
- In hold ball position, partner pushes your right shoulder
- Correct: Stable, rooted in left leg
- Incorrect: Wobbly or already committed forward

Phase 2: Step and Separate¶

Movement:
1. Inhale slightly
2. Step right foot forward (heel touches first)
3. Waist rotates to right
4. Right hand separates upward and outward
5. Left hand presses downward and backward
6. Weight shifts forward to right leg (70%)
7. Left foot follows, heel lifts naturally

Final Position:
- Right arm: Extended forward, palm diagonally up, shoulder height
- Left hand: Presses down beside left hip, palm down
- Right leg: 70% weight, knee bent
- Left leg: 30% weight, straight
- Waist: Faces diagonally right (45 degrees)
- Eyes: Look over right hand

Key Points:
- Step like cat: light, controlled, intentional
- Hand separation simultaneous with waist rotation
- Movement flows in one continuous arc
- Speed: 4-6 seconds for entire transition
- Feeling: Opening like a flower blooming

Common Mistakes:
❌ Foot stomps down
   → Correction: Place heel first, then roll entire foot down

❌ Hands move before waist
   → Correction: Initiate from dantian, hands follow

❌ Weight 50/50 in final position
   → Correction: Clearly 70% front, 30% back

❌ Right shoulder rises
   → Correction: Keep shoulders sunk, especially right

Testing:
- Partner pushes your extended right hand backward
- Correct: Force transmits to ground through structure
- Incorrect: Arm collapses or shoulder lifts

Phase 3: Settle and Root¶

Movement:
1. Exhale completely
2. Settle weight into right leg
3. Feel ground connection through entire right foot
4. Maintain peng in right arm
5. Left hand continues pressing down
6. Head lifts, tailbone drops (spine stretches)

Duration: Hold for 2-3 breaths

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Rooted like old tree
- Arm expands but doesn't tense
- Can step immediately in any direction
- Calm alertness

Left Wild Horse Parts Mane¶

Transition from Right: 

1. Shift weight 100% to right leg
2. Left foot turns out 45 degrees (weight still on right)
3. Right hand circles down, left hand circles up
4. Hands form "hold ball" position (reversed)
5. Step left foot forward
6. Separate hands with waist rotation to left
7. Mirror the right-side movement

Key Differences (Left vs Right): - Left hand rises (was right) - Right hand presses (was left) - Waist rotates left (was right) - Same principles, opposite direction

Training Emphasis: Most people have a dominant side. The weaker side needs more attention, not less. Practice left side 20% more than right until balance develops.

Third Repetition (Right Side)¶

Flow: Complete left side → Transition back to right → Third repetition

Why Odd Numbers? Traditional Chinese forms use odd repetitions (3, 5, 7) because: - Yang energy (active, expansive) - Allows time for learning and integration - Symmetry (begin and end on same side)

Breathing Pattern¶

Hold Ball (preparation):    Natural breath, slight inhalation
Step and Separate:          Exhale during movement
Settle and Root:            Complete exhalation, brief pause
Transition to other side:   Natural inhalation

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent approaches with straight punch

Application Sequence: 

1. Hold Ball: Prepare structure, assess distance
2. Step In: Close distance, enter opponent's space
3. Right Hand Rises: Deflect opponent's punching arm upward
4. Left Hand Presses: Control opponent's structure, prevent retreat
5. Body Rotation: Generate force from waist, not arms
6. Final Position: Opponent off-balance, you rooted and ready

Power Quality: Not pushing opponent away - disrupting their structure so they cannot resist

Country Tang's Teaching: "Like separating wild horse's mane - one hand lifts, one hand presses, horse loses balance and follows where you lead."

Common Errors Across All Three Repetitions¶

Error Effect Correction
Feet in straight line Unstable, easy to push sideways Keep feet shoulder-width apart laterally
Hands move too fast loses peng jin, becomes external Slow down, feel resistance
Waist doesn't rotate Arm strength only, no power Initiate from dantian, hands follow
Weight unclear Double-weighted, stuck Practice slower, feel each transition
Eyes look down Loses awareness, poor balance Eyes level, peripheral vision soft

Training Progression¶

Week 1-2: Learn Sequence¶

  • Practice rights side only: 10 repetitions
  • Practice left side only: 10 repetitions
  • Connect both sides: 5 complete cycles
  • Focus: Memorize movement pattern

Week 3-4: Refine Structure¶

  • Single repetitions with long pauses
  • Check each position in mirror
  • Focus: Alignment, weight distribution
  • Add: Partner testing for each position

Week 5-6: Integrate Waist¶

  • Hands follow waist, not lead
  • Slow practice: 10 seconds per transition
  • Focus: Origin of movement from dantian
  • Test: Partner applies light resistance

Week 7-8: Add Intent¶

  • Visualize opponent during practice
  • Sense energy flow in arms
  • Focus: Quality of peng jin
  • Result: Movement looks the same, feels different internally

Form 3: White Crane Spreads Wings (Bai He Liang Chi 白鹤亮翅)¶

Cultural Context¶

Symbolism: White crane represents longevity, elegance, and precise striking in Chinese martial arts

Visual Quality: One leg empty (crane stance), arms spread like wings - beautiful and martial

Key Training Focus: - Balance on single leg - Opposing forces (one hand up, one down) - Vertical alignment (head to foot in one line) - Empty/full distinction (100/0 weight distribution)

Movement Breakdown¶

Phase 1: Transition from Wild Horse¶

Starting Position: End of third Wild Horse Parts Mane (right side)

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to right leg
2. Left foot steps forward, heel touches
3. Right hand circles down in front of body
4. Left hand circles up in front of body
5. Hands cross at wrists (right over left)
6. Weight remains on right leg (preparation)

Hand Position:
- Right hand: Circles down from shoulder height to chest level
- Left hand: Circles up from hip height to chest level
- Arms create rounded shape (not straight lines)
- Eyes follow right hand initially

Phase 2: Rise and Open¶

Movement:
1. Inhale as hands continue circling
2. Right hand rises to right side, palm faces left
3. Left hand lowers to left side, palm faces down
4. Left foot becomes empty (toe touches ground)
5. Weight 100% on right leg
6. Arms spread like crane wings

Final Position:
- Right arm: Raised to head height, palm facing left (inward)
- Left hand: Presses down beside left hip, palm down
- Left leg: Empty, toe lightly touching ground
- Right leg: 100% weight, knee slightly bent
- Body: Upright, crown lifts, tailbone drops
- Eyes: Look straight ahead (not at hands)

Key Points:
- Opposing forces: Right hand up, left hand down
- No leaning: Spine vertical despite arm asymmetry
- Empty leg: Can lift immediately without weight shift
- Peng maintained: Arms rounded, not collapsed
- Speed: 6-8 seconds for entire transition

Common Mistakes:
❌ Leaning to left (counterbalance error)
   → Correction: Keep spine vertical, engage core

❌ Left foot bears weight
   → Correction: Practice lifting left foot 1 inch to verify emptiness

❌ Right shoulder rises with right hand
   → Correction: Drop right shoulder, elongate neck

❌ Arms straight (no peng)
   → Correction: Maintain rounded arm structure

Testing:
- Partner pushes your left shoulder sideways
- Correct: Doesn't collapse, stays upright
- Incorrect: Falls toward left, loses balance

Phase 3: Settle and Express¶

Movement:
1. Exhale completely
2. Feel ground connection through right foot
3. Expand through crown of head
4. Arms maintain peng without tension
5. Hold for 2-3 seconds, then transition

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Elegant like crane
-rooted yet ready to fly
- Calm alertness
- Whole-body stretch (head up, tailbone down)

Breathing Pattern¶

Transition (hands circle):    Natural breath
Rise and Open:                Inhalation (expansion)
Settle and Express:           Exhalation (rooting)
Hold:                         Brief pause (1-2 seconds)

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent attacks from front with grab or strike

Application: 

1. Transition: Hands circle to deflect incoming attack
2. Right Hand Rises: Lift opponent's arm, expose torso
3. Left Hand Presses: Control opponent's structure, prevent retreat
4. Empty Leg: Ready to kick (knee, groin, shin)
5. Posture: Beautiful but dangerous - like crane

Strategic Quality: Deceptive - looks like passive pose, actually ready to strike or kick instantly

Classical Text: "White Crane Spreads Wings - one hand rises to heaven, one hand presses to earth. Opponent cannot fathom whether you will strike above or below."

Balance Development Exercises¶

If You Struggle with This Form:

Exercise 1: Wall-Assisted Balance¶

1. Stand beside wall, fingertips lightly touching
2. Practice White Crane posture
3. Use wall only for micro-adjustments
4. Gradually reduce finger pressure
5. Goal: No wall contact needed

Exercise 2: Eyes-Closed Balance¶

1. Practice form near wall (safety)
2. Close eyes in final position
3. Hold for 10-20 seconds
4. Develop: Proprioception, not visual dependency
5. Progress: Increase duration to 1 minute

Exercise 3: Dynamic Balance¶

1. From White Crane position
2. Lift left knee to waist height
3. Hold for 3 breaths
4. Lower foot back to original position
5. Repeat: 5 times each side
6. Develops: Single-leg strength and control

Common Questions¶

Q: My standing leg shakes. Is this normal? A: Yes, initially. It means you're building strength and neuromuscular control. Shake-down occurs in 2-4 weeks of daily practice. If it persists beyond 6 weeks, check that you're not locking the knee (should be micro-bent).

Q: How do I prevent leaning? A: Imagine string from crown of head to ceiling. Simultaneously imagine tailbone heavy, dropping toward ground. This creates axial extension. Practice in front of mirror - if ear, shoulder, hip, ankle don't align, adjust.

Q: Should left foot touch ground at all? A: Yes, lightly. "Empty" doesn't mean "floating." Toe touches ground for proprioceptive feedback, but zero weight. Test: Partner should be able to slide paper under your left foot without resistance.

Form 4: Brush Knee and Push (Lou Xi Ao Bu 搂膝拗步) - 3 Repetitions¶

Movement Overview¶

Named After: Lower hand brushes past knee, upper hand pushes forward

Key Training Focus: - Coordinated hand and foot movement - Forward intention with rooted structure - Protecting lower body while attacking upper body - Stepping with complete weight transfer

Repetitions in 24-Form: Three times (left, right, left)

Martial Emphasis: Defend low, attack high - simultaneous defense and offense

Left Brush Knee and Push¶

Phase 1: Turn and Step (Preparation)¶

Starting Position: End of White Crane Spreads Wings

Movement:
1. Shift weight 100% to right leg
2. Left foot steps forward (heel first)
3. Right hand rises to right side of head
4. Left hand circles down and back
5. Weight remains on right leg (100%)
6. Left heel touches, toes point forward

Hand Positions:
- Right hand: Palm faces left, beside right ear (guard position)
- Left hand: Circles back behind left hip, palm faces down
- Eyes: Look forward (direction of intended movement)

Key Points:
- Step like cat: Light, controlled
- Hands move simultaneously with step
- Hips remain facing forward (not rotated yet)
- Feel like coiling spring (preparation for release)

Common Mistakes:
❌ Weight shifts to left foot too early
   → Correction: Keep weight back until brush/push phase

❌ Right hand too far from head
   → Correction: Palm should be 10-15cm from right ear

❌ Left hand disappears completely
   → Correction: Should remain in peripheral vision

Testing:
- In preparation, partner pushes your left shoulder
- Correct: Stable, can step back if needed
- Incorrect: Committed forward, can't change mind

Phase 2: Brush and Push (Execution)¶

Movement:
1. Inhale slightly (preparation)
2. Exhale as you execute
3. Left hand brushes past left knee (downward motion)
4. Right hand pushes forward from beside ear
5. Weight shifts forward to left leg (70%)
6. Waist rotates slightly left
7. Right foot follows (heel lifts)

Final Position:
- Left hand: Presses down past left knee, palm down
- Right hand: Pushes forward, palm out, shoulder height
- Left leg: 70% weight, knee bent
- Right leg: 30% weight, straight
- Waist: Faces forward (not rotated)
- Eyes: Look over right hand

Biomechanical Analysis:
- Left hand: Defensive action (blocks low attack)
- Right hand: Offensive action (strikes/pushes opponent)
- Simultaneous: Defense and offense occur together
- Power source: Leg drive + waist rotation, not arm strength

Key Points:
- Brush and push happen simultaneously
- Left hand brushes close to knee (not wide)
- Right hand pushes from centerline
- Weight shift completes with hand actions
- Speed: Brush/push takes 3-4 seconds

Common Mistakes:
❌ Brush hand moves too wide (away from body)
   → Correction: Hand should nearly graze knee

❌ Push hand uses only arm strength
   → Correction: Feel power from rear leg drive

❌ Weight 50/50 in final position
   → Correction: Clear 70/30 distribution

❌ Shoulders rise during push
   → Correction: Exhale fully, drop shoulders

Testing:
- Partner pushes your right hand backward
- Correct: Structure rooted, force transmits to ground
- Incorrect: Upper body moves independently from legs

Phase 3: Settle and Extend¶

Movement:
1. Complete exhalation
2. Feel extension through right hand
3. Left hand continues pressing down
4. Head lifts, tailbone drops
5. Hold for 1-2 seconds

Feeling to Cultivate:
- Forward intention without leaning
- Simultaneous defense (low) and offense (high)
- Power rooted in earth, expressed in hand
- Calm aggression (paradoxical but essential)

Right Brush Knee and Push¶

Transition from Left: 

1. Shift weight 100% to left leg
2. Right foot pivots outward 45 degrees (heel pivot)
3. Left hand circles down, right hand circles back
4. Step right foot forward
5. Mirror the left-side movement
6. Brush with right hand, push with left hand

Key Coordination: - Opposite hand and foot step together (left hand brushes, left foot steps - NO, it's contralateral: right hand pushes, left foot steps) - This is "Ao Bu" (awkward step) - opposite side coordination - Cross-body pattern develops whole-body integration

Third Repetition (Left Side)¶

Complete right side → Transition → Third left side → Hold final position for next form

Breathing Pattern¶

Preparation (turn/step):     Natural inhalation
Brush and Push:              Complete exhalation
Settle and Extend:           Brief pause (empty lungs)
Transition to other side:    Natural inhalation

Martial Application¶

Scenario: Opponent attacks with low kick or punch to lower body

Application Sequence: 

1. Preparation: Recognize incoming low attack
2. Step Forward: Close distance aggressively
3. Brush Hand: Deflect/sweep opponent's attacking limb
4. Push Hand: Simultaneous strike to opponent's chest/face
5. Weight Forward: Body mass behind strike
6. Result: Opponent's attack neutralized, structure broken

Power Quality: Not just hand push - entire body mass moves forward with structure

Combat Principle: "Attack what is attacking you." Opponent's low attack creates high opening - exploit immediately.

Common Errors and Corrections¶

Error Effect Correction Method
Brush hand too far from knee Loses defensive coverage, wastes movement Practice slowly, feel hand-knee relationship
Push hand before weight shift No power, just arm movement Coordinate: leg drive → waist → hand
Looking down at brush hand Loses awareness of opponent Eyes forward, peripheral vision
Back foot flat (not following) Disconnected, loses power Right heel lifts naturally as weight shifts
Hips rotate too much Power dissipates sideways Hips face forward, power goes straight

Training Drills¶

Drill 1: Wall Push Integration¶

Purpose: Develop whole-body pushing power

Setup:
1. Stand facing wall (arm's length distance)
2. Assume brush knee and push position
3. Place right palm on wall (shoulder height)

Execution:
1. From rear leg, drive forward
2. Feel force transmit through structure to wall
3. Wall doesn't move - you're testing your mechanics
4. Push at 30%, 50%, 70% intensity
5. Focus: Quality of force transmission

Repetitions: 10 each side
Frequency: Daily

Drill 2: Partner Resistance¶

Purpose: Test structure under pressure

Setup:
1. You perform brush knee and push
2. Partner places palm against your pushing hand
3. Partner applies light backward pressure

Execution:
1. Maintain structure
2. Don't collapse or resist with arm
3. Feel pressure transmit to ground
4. If partner increases pressure, step back and reset

Benefit: Immediate feedback on structural integrity

Drill 3: Slow-Motion Coordination¶

Purpose: Develop precise hand-foot timing

Execution:
1. Perform form in extreme slow motion (20 seconds per repetition)
2. Notice when hand and foot coordinate
3. Identify any hesitations or rushes
4. Smooth out transitions

Insight: What feels painfully slow in practice is correct speed for learning

Training Progression (4 Weeks)¶

Week 1: Pattern Recognition¶

  • Learn movement sequence
  • Practice each side 10 times
  • Focus: Memorization, basic flow

Week 2: Weight Transfer Clarity¶

  • Feel 100% → 70/30 transitions
  • Pause in each position
  • Focus: Clear yin/yang distinction

Week 3: Whole-Body Integration¶

  • Coordinate brush/push with leg drive
  • Power from legs, not arms
  • Focus: Origin of movement from dantian

Week 4: Intent and Application¶

  • Visualize opponent
  • Add martial purpose to movements
  • Focus: Quality of energy (not quantity)

Common Questions¶

Q: Which is more important - brush or push? A: Both are equally important and simultaneous. The brush protects while the push attacks. In application, they're two halves of one action. Training emphasis: Perfect the brush first (defense enables offense).

Q: My lower back hurts after practicing. What's wrong? A: Likely you're leaning forward or arching your back. Correction: Keep spine vertical, engage lower abdominals slightly (tuck tailbone), rise from crown. If pain persists, reduce depth of stance and consult teacher.

Q: Should I feel power in my pushing hand? A: You should feel connection/grounding, not local muscle fatigue. If your arm or shoulder tires quickly, you're using li (muscle) instead of jin (whole-body force). Correct by: Slowing down, relaxing more, initiating from legs.

Summary: Forms 1-8 Progress Path¶

These first four forms establish the foundation for all 24 forms:

Beginning Form: Stillness, grounding, peng development Wild Horse Parts Mane: Weight transfer, waist rotation, opening/closing White Crane Spreads Wings: Balance, vertical alignment, opposing forces Brush Knee and Push: Hand-foot coordination, forward intention, simultaneous defense/offense

Next Forms (5-8): - Playing the Pipa: Compact structure, upward intention - Repulse Monkey: Retreat while maintaining structure, backward stepping - Diagonal Flying: Large expansion, diagonal power - Wave Hands Like Clouds: Continuous flow, waist-driven arm waves

Practice Recommendation: Master these four forms before moving to Forms 5-8. Depth over breadth builds lasting skill.

Continue to Forms 9-16 for intermediate movements including Snake Creeps Down, Golden Rooster Stands on One Leg, and Fair Lady Works at Shuttles.
