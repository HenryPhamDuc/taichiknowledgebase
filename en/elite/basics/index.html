    Elite Basics¶

    What This Section Is¶

    Most students never reach this level. Not because they're not talented, but because Elite practice asks for something most people don't have: time. Years of consistent Foundation and Advanced work, the patience to repeat the same drill for a decade, and the humility to keep finding new layers in movements you thought you already understood.

    If you've made it here, you've earned it. The material below assumes years of daily practice, comfort with a complete form, and meaningful partner work. This isn't beginner content. It's not even advanced content. This is the work you do when the form has become a place you live, not something you do.

    I get asked: "How long does it take to reach Elite level?" My honest answer: if you have to ask, you're not there yet. Elite isn't a goal. It's a recognition. You don't decide you're at Elite; you discover one day that you've been living there for years.

    What You'll Work On¶

    Advanced Internal Mechanics¶

    At this level, every movement is a question: where is the energy coming from, and what is it doing? You start to notice that what you thought was a single movement is actually three or four smaller movements, each with its own internal logic.

    You'll work on:

    • Distinguishing substantial from empty in real time — not as a concept, but as a felt sense that updates with every step
    • Opening and closing the kua — the hip joints that govern whether power moves through your center or gets trapped in your legs
    • Micro-adjustments in standing meditation — small shifts that change the entire quality of your posture without changing its shape
    • Connecting the three dantians — lower, middle, and upper energy centers working as one system

    Fajin Training¶

    Fajin (發勁), often translated as "issuing power," is the explosive expression of internal structure. It's the moment when a slow, gentle Tai Chi movement can deliver force that surprises everyone — including the person doing it.

    Fajin isn't a technique. It's the visible result of years of structural training. When your structure is correct, fajin is what happens naturally. When your structure is wrong, no amount of training will produce it.

    The work here is largely structural: fixing the kua, opening the back, connecting the shoulders to the waist, learning to move from the dantian instead of the limbs. The "explosive" part comes last, and only after the structural work is solid.

    Silk Reeling Exercises¶

    Silk reeling (纏絲功, chán sī gōng) is the foundational exercise of Chen-style Tai Chi. It's a set of spiraling movements that train the body to move as a connected whole, with every joint participating in every direction of motion.

    You'll work on:

    • Single silk reeling in all directions
    • Combined silk reeling with weight transfer
    • Silk reeling in stance transitions
    • Silk reeling at speed (the form looks like a slow, continuous spiral)

    Chen-style masters consider silk reeling the foundation of everything else. Even if you practice Yang style, the body mechanics developed through silk reeling will transform your form.

    Standing Meditation at Depth¶

    Wuji stance (站樁, zhàn zhuāng) is the most boring and most important exercise in Tai Chi. Standing still, doing nothing, for extended periods.

    At Foundation level, you learn to stand. At Advanced level, you learn to stand well. At Elite level, you stand for an hour at a time and discover that "doing nothing" was an illusion — there's always something to feel, something to release, something to notice.

    Progression:

    • 5 minutes: Foundation warm-up
    • 20 minutes: Advanced practice
    • 45-60 minutes: Elite depth work
    • 90+ minutes: Teacher-level practice (some masters stand for two hours)

    The benefits at this level aren't physical anymore. They're perceptual. You start to notice your thinking. You start to notice your noticing. Standing meditation becomes a mirror.

    Prerequisites¶

    You should be able to answer yes to most of these before you start this section:

    • I have practiced consistently for at least three years
    • I can perform a complete long form from memory without thinking
    • I have a regular push hands partner and we've trained together for at least a year
    • I understand fajin as a concept and have felt it at least once, even if I can't reliably produce it
    • I can stand in Wuji for 20 minutes without significant discomfort
    • I have a teacher I can ask questions of, even if only occasionally
    • I'm not in a rush. I have years, not weeks.

    If most of these are no, that's not a problem — it just means Elite work isn't for you yet. The Foundation and Advanced sections are still full of depth to discover.

    How to Approach This Material¶

    Get a teacher. At this level, the articles can point you in the right direction, but you need someone who can correct your structure in real time. Books and videos are maps. A teacher is a guide who knows the territory.

    Practice less, but deeper. The temptation is to add more exercises. Resist it. Two or three deep drills practiced daily for a year will take you further than twenty drills practiced superficially.

    Document your practice. Internal development is invisible. Keep a journal. Note what you felt, what changed, what surprised you. Years from now, this becomes your map of progress.

    Accept that you don't know. Elite practitioners share one quality: they've stopped pretending they understand what's happening. They know there's always another layer.

    What's Next¶

    When you're ready, the Elite Deep Dives section explores:

    There's no rush. There never is.
