    Tai Chi Glossary¶

    This glossary contains Chinese terms, pronunciation, and characters used throughout the Tai Chi Knowledge Base. Terms are organized by category for quick reference.

    The 10 Essentials¶

    The original Chinese names for Yang Chengfu's 10 Essentials, with pronunciation and character breakdown.

    English Pinyin Wade-Giles Characters Literal Meaning
    1. Empty, Nimble, and Alert Xū Líng Dǐng Jìn Hsu Ling Ting Chin 虛靈頂勁 "Empty spirit reaches the crown"
    2. Contain the Chest, Raise the Back Hán Xiōng Bá Bèi Han Hsiung Pa Pei 含胸拔背 "Hold chest, pull up back"
    3. Relax the Waist Sōng Yāo Sung Yao 松腰 "Loosen the waist"
    4. Distinguish Substantial and Insubstantial Fēn Qīng Xū Shí Fen Ching Hsu Shih 分清虛實 "Separate clear empty-full"
    5. Sink Shoulders, Drop Elbows Chén Jiān Zhuì Zhǒu Ch'en Chien Chui Chou 沉肩墜肘 "Sink shoulders, drop elbows"
    6. Use Intent, Not Force Yòng Yì Bù Yòng Lì Yung I Pu Yung Li 用意不用力 "Use mind, not strength"
    7. Coordinate Upper and Lower Shàng Xià Xiāng Suí Shang Hsia Hsiang Sui 上下相隨 "Above-below follow each other"
    8. Internal and External United Nèi Wài Xiāng Hé Nei Wai Hsiang Ho 內外相合 "Inside-outside unite together"
    9. Continuous, Unbroken Xiāng Lián Bù Duàn Hsiang Lien Pu Tuan 相連不斷 "Connected without breaking"
    10. Seek Stillness in Movement Dòng Zhōng Qiú Jìng Tung Chung Ch'iu Ching 動中求靜 "In motion seek stillness"

    Notes on the 10 Essentials (十要 - Shí Yào)¶

    • 十要 (Shí Yào): literally "Ten Requirements" or "Ten Essentials"
    • Codified by Yang Chengfu (楊澄甫, 1883-1936)
    • Third-generation master of Yang-style Tai Chi
    • These principles distinguish internal martial arts from external styles

    Pronunciation Guide¶

    Pinyin Basics¶

    • Q = sounds like "ch" (but sharper): qi ≈ "chee"
    • X = sounds like "sh" (but softer): xi ≈ "shee"
    • Zh = sounds like "j" in "judge": zhang ≈ "jahng"
    • C = sounds like "ts": cai ≈ "tsai"
    • Z = sounds like "dz": zai ≈ "dzai"
    • Ü = like German "ü" or French "u": lü (pursed lips, say "ee")

    Tone Marks¶

    • First tone (ā): high, level tone
    • Second tone (á): rising tone (like asking a question)
    • Third tone (ǎ): low, dipping tone
    • Fourth tone (à): falling, sharp tone (like a command)
    • Neutral tone (a): light, quick tone
