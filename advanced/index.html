Advanced Level¶

Where You Are Now¶

If you're reading this, you've done the Foundation work. You know the form. Your posture holds without thinking. You can stand in Wuji for ten minutes and not be distracted. The movements feel familiar.

Good. Now the real work begins.

Advanced isn't about learning new forms. It's about doing the same forms with finer awareness. The movements don't change. What changes is the depth at which you understand them. The same Ward Off that took you six months to learn is now something you can spend years refining.

I get asked often: "What's the difference between Advanced and Foundation?" Here's the honest answer: in Foundation, you're learning the what. In Advanced, you're learning the how. In Elite, you're learning the why. Each level is the same movements. Each level is also completely different work.

What Makes This "Advanced"?¶

At this level, you're no longer learning what to do — you're refining how you do it. The movements are familiar. Now we work with:

Energy flow (Qi). Feeling and directing internal movement. This isn't mystical — it's the felt sense of weight, pressure, and intention moving through your structure. Beginners don't notice it. Advanced practitioners can feel it clearly.

Timing and rhythm. When to be fast, when to be slow, when to be heavy, when to be light. The form has a tempo, but the tempo isn't constant. Different parts of the form call for different qualities.

Application. The martial meaning behind each movement. "Wild Horse Parts Mane" sounds poetic. It also describes a specific technique for redirecting force. The form contains a library of self-defense applications, and at the Advanced level, you start to see them.

Integration. Connecting form with function. The form isn't a sequence to memorize; it's a system for moving, fighting, and being. At this level, the form starts to inform everything you do — how you stand, how you walk, how you respond to pressure.

Prerequisites¶

Before diving into Advanced material, make sure you can honestly say yes to these:

  • Comfortable with all Foundation basics. The 10 Essentials feel natural. The form is memorized. You don't have to think about what's next.
  • Can demonstrate proper posture without thinking. When you stand in Wuji, your body knows what to do. You don't have to remember the cues.
  • Have established a daily practice habit (even 10 minutes). Consistency matters more than duration. A daily 15-minute practice is more valuable than a weekly 2-hour session.
  • Understand the 10 Essentials at an intellectual level. You can name them, describe what they mean, and recognize when you're violating them in your own practice.

If any of these are no, that's not a problem. It just means you have more Foundation work to do. The Foundation section is always here. There's no shame in returning to it. The masters do.

How to Approach This Material¶

Find a partner. Push hands and partner work require another body. Without a partner, you'll be guessing at half the material. Local clubs, online communities, weekend workshops — find a way to train with someone.

Slow down. The temptation at this level is to add more — more forms, more techniques, more exercises. Resist it. The form you know is deeper than you realize. Spend three months refining one movement before moving to the next.

Keep a journal. Internal development is invisible. Note what you felt, what worked, what surprised you. The journal becomes your record of progress.

Return to Foundation monthly. Advanced material exposes every weakness in your basics. When you find a weakness, go back and rebuild it. This isn't regression. This is how the work deepens.

A Note on Skepticism¶

You'll encounter claims in the Advanced material that sound strange. "Move from your center." "Yield to force." "Four ounces defeats a thousand pounds." These phrases mean something precise, but the precision is felt, not heard.

When something doesn't make sense, set it aside. Keep practicing. Come back to it in a few months. The same sentence that read like nonsense will one day read like a precise technical description of what your body just did.

This is normal. Internal arts are learned by doing, not by understanding them intellectually. The articles are maps. The territory is your practice.
