# Ian Sinclair's Advanced Applications Series

## Overview

The Advanced Applications series represents the culmination of Tai Chi training, where health practice and martial application converge. This guide covers push hands skills, fa jin (power issuance), sensitivity development, and the martial interpretation of form movements.

**What This Series Covers:**
- Push hands principles and progressive training methods
- Fa jin development: From structure to explosive power
- Sensitivity skills: Listening, understanding, responding
- Martial applications of common form movements
- Advanced testing and training approaches

**Who This Is For:**
- Practitioners with 1+ years of consistent form practice
- Students interested in martial applications
- Teachers seeking advanced training methods
- Anyone wanting to understand Tai Chi as complete art (health + martial)

## Part 1: Push Hands Foundations

### What Is Push Hands (Tui Shou)?

**Definition:** Two-person training method for developing sensitivity, rooting, and proper force application

**Purpose:**
- Test and refine structure under pressure
- Develop sensitivity to partner's intent and force
- Learn to yield and redirect without collapsing
- Bridge gap between form practice and application
- Cultivate ting jin (listening energy)

> **Ian's Teaching:** "Push hands is not about pushing harder. It's about feeling your own stuck points, understanding your partner's structure, and finding the path of least resistance. It's a conversation, not a competition."

---

### The Four Pillars of Push Hands

#### 1. Peng (Ward Off) - Maintaining Structure

**Purpose:** Maintain expansive force without resistance

**In Practice:**
```
When partner pushes:
- Don't collapse (lose peng)
- Don't resist (use opposite force)
- Instead: Maintain structure, redirect force through legs
```

**Training Drill:**
```
Exercise: Static Peng Maintenance
1. Partners in horse stance facing each other
2. Forearms in contact
3. Partner A applies light pressure
4. Partner B maintains peng without pushing back
5. Feel pressure travel to ground
6. Hold for 1 minute, switch
```

**Common Mistake:** Pushing back with arm strength (his li) instead of maintaining peng structure (peng jin)

---

#### 2. Lu (Roll Back) - Yielding and Redirecting

**Purpose:** Neutralize incoming force by rotating and leading

**Biomechanics:**
```
Force Redirection Sequence:
1. Feel partner's force direction (ting)
2. Rotate waist 45-90 degrees
3. Arms follow waist rotation
4. Partner's force continues past you
5. Partner loses balance forward
```

**Key Principle:** Don't pull partner - just remove your resistance and rotate

**Training Drill:**
```
Exercise: Single-Hand Lu
1. Partners face each other in bow stance
2. Right hands contact at wrist
3. Partner pushes forward
4. You rotate waist left, hand follows rotation
5. Partner's force goes past you into empty space
6. Practice: 2 minutes each side
```

**Common Mistake:** Pulling with arm. This alerts partner and creates tension. Just rotate waist and let hand follow.

---

#### 3. Ji (Press) - Compressive Force

**Purpose:** Issue forward force after yielding

**Biomechanics:**
```
Ji Sequence:
1. Partner pushes → you yield (Lu)
2. Partner over-commits forward
3. You rotate waist back to center
4. Both hands move forward together
5. Force generated from leg drive, not arms
```

**Key Point:** Ji happens after partner has lost structure from over-commitment

**Training Drill:**
```
Exercise: Lu-Ji Combination
1. Start with single-hand contact
2. Partner pushes → you Lu (rotate away)
3. As partner follows and commits → you Ji (press forward)
4. Use both hands, whole-body drive
5. Flow continuously: push → lu → ji → repeat
6. Practice: 3 minutes
```

---

#### 4. An (Push) - Downward-Forward Force

**Purpose:** Issue force with downward-then-forward vector

**Difference from Ji:**
- Ji: Direct forward compression
- An: Downward first, then forward (like waving motion)

**Application:**
```
Scenario: Partner's arms are blocking your ji
Solution: Use an to collapse their structure first

Execution:
1. Hands separate and move down (partner's arms follow)
2. Partner's structure compresses
3. Hands redirect forward as partner is "empty"
4. Issue from legs
```

**Training Drill:**
```
Exercise: Jin-An Combination
1. Begin with ward position
2. Jin (press forward) - partner resists
3. Separate hands, move down (an, part 1)
4. Partner's arms follow down
5. Rotate waist, issue forward (an, part 2)
6. Practice: 2 minutes, switch
```

### Training Progression for Push Hands

#### Level 1: Fixed-Step (Months 1-3)

**Format:**
- Feet planted, no stepping
- Single-hand contact or double-hand
- Focus on upper body skills

**Skills Developed:**
- Structure maintenance under pressure
- Basic peng jin
- Waist rotation timing
- Sensitivity to force direction

**Practice Structure:**
```
Week 1-4: Single-hand fixed pattern
- Partner A pushes, Partner B yields
- Predetermined pattern (push → lu → ji → an)
- Very slow, focus on mechanics

Week 5-8: Double-hand fixed pattern
- Both hands in contact
- Same predetermined pattern
- Add weight transfer awareness

Week 9-12: Fixed-step with variation
- Same pattern but change timing
- Add pauses, accelerations
- Begin light "feeling" for partner's intent
```

---

#### Level 2: Moving-Step (Months 4-6)

**Format:**
- Stepping allowed (forward, backward, sideways)
- Hands may lose/re-establish contact
- Focus on footwork integration

**Skills Developed:**
- Rooting while moving
- Weight transfer with direction changes
- Maintaining peng jin during transitions
- Closing/opening distance appropriately

**Practice Structure:**
```
Month 4: Step-back Lu
- Partner pushes → you step back + Lu
- Focus: Maintain structure while retreating

Month 5: Step-in Ji
- Create opening → step forward + Ji
- Focus: Issue power while advancing

Month 6: Directional changes
- 360-degree movement allowed
- Focus: Rooting in all directions, pivoting
```

---

#### Level 3: Free Push Hands (Months 7-12)

**Format:**
- No predetermined patterns
- Any technique allowed (within safety)
- Competitive or cooperative

**Skills Developed:**
- Spontaneous response to unknown force
- Strategy and timing
- Fa jin in dynamic context
- Sensitivity at higher speeds

**Practice Approaches:**

**Cooperative (Learning Mode):**
```
Goal: Help each other learn
- Work slowly, focus on correct response
- Provide clear feedback about what works
- No "winning" - mutual development
```

**Competitive (Testing Mode):**
```
Goal: Test skills under pressure
- Full resistance, realistic timing
- Keep score or play until off-balance
- Use to identify training gaps
- Always debrief after to learn
```

### Advanced Push Hands Concepts

#### Concept 1: Sticky Jin (Nian Jin)

**Definition:** Maintaining contact sensitivity throughout exchange

**Quality:** Like glue - never loses connection, always follows partner

**Training:**
```
Exercise: No-Loss Contact
1. Free push hands with rule: can't break contact
2. If contact is lost, both pause and reset
3. Focus: Maintain light but consistent pressure
4. Develop: Constant awareness of partner's structure

Duration: 3-5 minutes continuous
```

---

#### Concept 2: Listening Jin (Ting Jin)

**Definition:** Ability to feel partner's intent through contact

**What You're Sensing:**
- Direction of force
- Amount of pressure
- Speed of movement
- Weight distribution
- Tension or relaxation
- Intent to push, pull, redirect

**Development Exercise:**
```
Exercise: Eyes-Closed Sensitivity
1. Partners in contact, slow pattern
2. You close eyes completely
3. Focus entirely on tactile sensation
4. Identify partner's intent before movement happens
5. Respond based only on feeling, not vision

Duration: 2 minutes each
Effect: Dramatically increases sensitivity
```

---

#### Concept 3: Understanding Jin (Dong Jin)

**Definition:** Comprehending partner's structure and finding weakness

**Beyond Feeling:**
- Ting (listening): "I feel force"
- Dong (understanding): "I know why this force is weak"

**What You're Understanding:**
- Which joint is blocking force transmission
- Where partner is double-weighted
- Which direction partner cannot resist
- When partner is committed and cannot change

**Development Exercise:**
```
Exercise: Weakness Detection
1. Slow free push hands
2. Partner maintains peng structure
3. You explore with light pressure in different directions
4. Find direction where partner cannot resist
5. Identify structural cause parallel to balcony

Duration: 5 minutes
```

---

#### Concept 4: Neutralizing Jin (Hua Jin)

**Definition:** Dissolving incoming force so it has no effect

**Methods:**
1. **Rotation:** Waist turns, force goes past
2. **Sinking:** Body lowers, force goes into ground
3. **Yielding:** Structure compresses slightly, absorbs force
4. **Leading:** Guide force into emptiness

**Training Exercise:**
```
Exercise: Force Neutralization Drill
1. Partner applies steady forward pressure
2. You practice one neutralizing method
3. Partner confirms force has "disappeared"
4. Switch method and repeat

Duration: 5 minutes, rotate methods
```

## Part 2: Fa Jin (Power Issuance)

### Understanding Fa Jin

**Definition:** Explosive issuance of whole-body force

**Characteristics:**
- Sudden acceleration after slow preparation
- Whole-body coordination
- Brief duration (milliseconds)
- Followed by immediate relaxation
- Penetrating quality (not surface impact)

> **Classical Text:** "Fa jin should be like a whip crack or a cannon firing - sudden, explosive, and gone immediately after."

---

### Types of Fa Jin

#### Type 1: Long Jin (Chang Jin)

**Quality:** Extended, penetrating force

**Application:**
- Long-distance pushes
- Throws that send opponent flying
- Through-force (impact continues past surface)

**Training:**
```
Exercise: Candle Blow
1. Light candle 3-4 feet away
2. Issue jin toward candle
3. Goal: Extinguish flame without moving candle
4. Quality: Focused, penetrating force

Practice: 10-20 repetitions
```

---

#### Type 2: Short Jin (Duan Jin)

**Quality:** Quick, close-range force

**Application:**
- Close-quarter strikes
- Sudden issuing from contact distance
- "Inch power" (one-inch punch)

**Training:**
```
Exercise: Wall Tap Fa Jin
1. Stand 6 inches from wall, palm on surface
2. Without winding up, issue force into wall
3. Feel ground drive through structure
4. Sound should be sharp "crack"

Practice: 10 reps each hand
```

---

#### Type 3: Trembling Jin (Zhan Dou Jin)

**Quality:** Vibrating, shaking force

**Application:**
- Disrupting opponent's structure
- Breaking through guarding arms
- Internal damage without external impact

**Training:**
```
Exercise: Body Vibration
1. Stand wu chi stance
2. Begin subtle trembling from legs
3. Let vibration travel up through body
4. Hands visibly shake without muscle tension
5. Stop, relax, repeat

Duration: 1-2 minutes
```

---

### Fa Jin Mechanics

#### Sequential Activation (Complete Breakdown)

**Timing Analysis (milliseconds):**
```
T=0ms:    Ground contact increases (rooting)
T=20ms:   Rear leg drives forward
T=40ms:   Knee extends, hip rotates
T=60ms:   Pelvis rotates, waist twists
T=80ms:   Lumbar spine rotates
T=100ms:  Thoracic spine accelerates rotation
T=120ms:  Shoulder girdle activates
T=140ms:  Arm accelerates forward
T=160ms:  Elbow extends (if striking)
T=180ms:  Wrist aligns, fist/hand contacts
T=200ms:  Force transmitted to target

Total: ~200ms from initiation to impact
```

**Key Points:**
- Each joint accelerates then decelerates
- Peak force occurs at moment of impact
- All muscles relax immediately after
- Breathing: Sharp exhalation on contact

---

### Fa Jin Training Progression

#### Phase 1: Structure Development (Months 1-2)

**Purpose:** Build foundation for power transmission

**Exercises:**
```
1. Standing Postures: 10-15 minutes daily
   - Wu chi: 5 minutes
   - Peng stance: 5 minutes each side
   
2. Slow Form with Intent: 15 minutes
   - Practice form slowly
   - Imagine issuing power at specific points
   - Don't actually issue - just mental intent
   
3. Wall Push Strength: 5 minutes
   - Horse stance, forearms on wall
   - Issue light force (30-50%)
   - Focus on whole-body coordination
```

---

#### Phase 2: Sequential Timing (Months 3-4)

**Purpose:** Develop correct joint firing sequence

**Exercises:**
```
1. Slow-Motion Fa Jin: 10 minutes
   - Single movement (e.g., single whip push)
   - Execute in 5 seconds
   - Feel each joint activate in sequence
   - Progress: 10 slow, 10 normal speed
   
2. Partner Resistance: 5 minutes
   - Partner applies light resistance
   - Issue force progressively (30%, 50%, 70%)
   - Focus on maintaining sequence
   
3. Fa Jin in Form: 15 minutes
   - Select 3-4 form movements with clear issuing
   - Issue power at end of each movement
   - Immediately relax after issuance
```

---

#### Phase 3: Speed and Power (Months 5-6)

**Purpose:** Develop explosive quality and increase force output

**Exercises:**
```
1. Explosive Fa Jin: 10 minutes
   - Single movements, maximum speed
   - Full power issuance (90-100%)
   - 10 repetitions, full rest between
   - Focus: Sharp, sudden, relaxed after
   
2. Target Practice: 10 minutes
   - Heavy bag or wall pad
   - Issue with full power
   - Listen for sound quality (crack vs thud)
   - Track: 50-100 strikes per session
   
3. Continuous Issuance: 5 minutes
   - Issue, retract, issue again
   - 2-3 times per second
   - Stay relaxed between issuances
   - Develop: Rapid-fire capability
```

---

#### Phase 4: Application Integration (Months 7-12)

**Purpose:** Use fa jin in dynamic, responsive context

**Exercises:**
```
1. Push Hands with Fa Jin: 15 minutes
   - Free push hands
   - Issue when opportunity appears
   - Partner provides realistic resistance
   - Focus: Timing and opportunity recognition
   
2. Form Applications: 15 minutes
   - Practice form movements with martial intent
   - Visualize opponent
   - Issue power at application points
   - Combine: Yield → Neutralize → Issue
   
3. Equipment Training: 10 minutes
   - Heavy bag: Power development
   - Focus mitts: Accuracy + timing
   - Wall pad: Structure testing
```

---

### Common Fa Jin Mistakes

| Mistake | Symptom | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Muscle tension before issuing | Slow, telegraphed, fatiguing | Stay relaxed until moment of impact |
| Wind-up motion | Partner sees it coming | Issue from stillness, no preparation |
| Using arm strength only | Weak force, local fatigue | Drive from legs, transmit through structure |
| Holding breath | Tension throughout, quick fatigue | Sharp exhalation on contact |
| Not relaxing after | Slow recovery, can't issue again | Immediately return to song state |
| Poor timing | Issues when partner is rooted | Wait for partner's moment of imbalance |

## Part 3: Sensitivity Development

### The Four Levels of Sensitivity

#### Level 1: Physical Touch (Ting - Listening)

**What It Is:**
- Feeling pressure, direction, speed through skin contact
- Basic proprioception of partner's movement
- Foundation for all sensitivity skills

**Development Exercise:**
```
Exercise: Pressure Tracking
1. Partners in contact, eyes closed
2. Partner A moves hand in random directions (slow)
3. Partner B follows with hand, maintaining light contact
4. Switch roles after 2 minutes

Focus: Never lose contact, don't push or pull
```

---

#### Level 2: Intent Recognition (Dong - Understanding)

**What It Is:**
- Sensing partner's intention before movement
- Predicting direction of force before it manifests
- Reading changes in pressure and tension

**Development Exercise:**
```
Exercise: Intention Reading
1. Partners in contact, very slow
2. Partner A forms intent to push (but doesn't push yet)
3. Partner B says "now" when they feel intent form
4. Partner A pushes, Partner B yields

Progression:
- Week 1: Say "now" after push begins
- Week 2: Say "now" as push starts (simultaneous)
- Week 3: Say "now" before push starts (anticipation)
```

---

#### Level 3: Structural Understanding (Hua - Neutralizing)

**What It Is:**
- Identifying weak points in partner's structure
- Knowing which direction partner cannot resist
- Timing neutralization to partner's commitment

**Development Exercise:**
```
Exercise: Weakness Exploration
1. Partner maintains peng structure
2. You apply light pressure in 8 directions (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW)
3. Identify direction where partner cannot resist
4. Identify structural reason (e.g., knee alignment, weight distribution)
5. Exploit by issuing in weak direction

Duration: 5 minutes each partner
```

---

#### Level 4: Spontaneous Response (Ying - Responding)

**What It Is:**
- Automatic, correct response without conscious thought
- Flow state in push hands
- "Don't think - just do"

**Development Exercise:**
```
Exercise: No-Thinking Push Hands
1. Free push hands, realistic speed
2. Your goal: Don't think at all
3. When partner pushes, don't decide - just respond
4. If you catch yourself thinking, say "thinking" aloud
5. Partner tracks how often you think

Target: Reduce "thinking" calls over time
```

## Part 4: Martial Applications of Form Movements

### Application 1: Grasp Bird's Tail

#### Ward Left (Peng Left)

**Martial Application:**
- Deflect incoming strike to left
- Simultaneously strike with left forearm
- Right hand guards center

**Training Drill:**
```
Scenario: Partner throws right cross
1. You rotate waist left
2. Left forearm deflects strike outward
3. Right hand controls partner's elbow
4. Issue peng jin to off-balance partner

Practice: 2 minutes, switch
```

---

#### Roll Back (Lu)

**Martial Application:**
- Yield to opponent's forward force
- Lead opponent into over-commitment
- Setup for counter-attack

**Training Drill:**
```
Scenario: Partner pushes both hands forward
1. You rotate waist 90 degrees
2. Arms follow rotation, leading partner's force past
3. Partner stumbles forward (off-balance)
4. You are positioned to strike or throw

Practice: 3 minutes, focus on timing
```

---

#### Press (Ji)

**Martial Application:**
- Strike with both hands after yielding
- Compress opponent's structure
- Issue penetrating force

**Training Drill:**
```
Scenario: After Lu, partner is over-committed
1. You rotate waist back to center
2. Both hands press forward together
3. Issue fa jin into partner's chest
4. Partner flies backward

Practice: 2 minutes
```

---

#### Push (An)

**Martial Application:**
- Collapse opponent's guard then strike
- Downward force followed by forward strike
- Targets: Chin, chest, solar plexus

**Training Drill:**
```
Scenario: Partner's arms block your ji
1. Hands separate, move downward
2. Partner's arms follow down (guard collapses)
3. You redirect hands forward
4. Issue fa jin to exposed target

Practice: 3 minutes
```

---

### Application 2: Single Whip

**Martial Application:**
- Hook hand traps opponent's arm
- Palm strikes opponent's face or chest
- Whole-body rotation generates power

**Training Drill:**
```
Scenario: Partner throws right punch
1. You intercept with left hand (outside block)
2. Right hand forms hook, traps partner's arm
3. Rotate waist forcefully to right
4. Left palm strikes partner's face/ribs
5. Issue fa jin at end of rotation

Practice: 2 minutes each side
```

---

### Application 3: White Crane Spreads Wings

**Martial Application:**
- Rising hand lifts opponent's arm
- Lowering hand controls opponent's structure
- Ready to kick with empty leg

**Training Drill:**
```
Scenario: Partner grabs your right wrist
1. Right arm rises (lifts partner's arm)
2. Left hand presses down on partner's elbow
3. Partner's structure breaks (arm locked in painful position)
4. Optional: Left foot kicks partner's knee or groin

Practice: 2 minutes, switch
```

---

### Application 4: Brush Knee and Push

**Martial Application:**
- Lower hand blocks/guards against low attacks
- Upper hand strikes opponent's face
- Forward step adds power

**Training Drill:**
```
Scenario: Partner throws low kick or punch
1. Lower hand brushes down (deflects attack)
2. Front leg steps forward
3. Upper palm strikes partner's chin or chest
4. Issue fa jin, drive partner backward

Practice: 3 minutes, switch sides
```

---

### Application 5: Cloud Hands

**Martial Application:**
- Continuous warding against multiple strikes
- Moving defense creates opening for counter
- Side-to-side movement evades attacks

**Training Drill:**
```
Scenario: Partner throws multiple punches
1. Use cloud hands movement side-to-side
2. Each hand wards incoming strikes
3. Movement evades attack line
4. When partner over-commits → issue counter

Practice: 3 minutes continuous movement
```

## Part 5: Advanced Training Methods

### Method 1: Eyes-Closed Training

**Purpose:**
- Eliminate visual dependency
- Amplify tactile sensitivity
- Develop true listening jin

**Exercises:**
```
1. Eyes-Closed Form: 10 minutes
   - Perform entire form with eyes closed
   - Focus on internal sensation of movement
   - Develop: Proprioception and balance

2. Eyes-Closed Push Hands: 5 minutes
   - Single-hand, eyes closed
   - Respond only to tactile input
   - Develop: Pure sensitivity

Warning: Not for competitive practice - safety concern
```

---

### Method 2: Variable Resistance Training

**Purpose:**
- Develop sensitivity to force changes
- Learn to adapt technique to different opponents
- Build responsive power

**Exercise:**
```
Push Hands with Variable Pressure:
Level 1: Light pressure (30%) - fast, technical
Level 2: Medium pressure (60%) - balanced
Level 3: Heavy pressure (90%) - structural integrity
Level 4: Non-stop (continuous change) - adaptive

Each level: 3 minutes
```

---

### Method 3: Multiple Opponent Training

**Purpose:**
- Develop 360-degree awareness
- Train rapid switching between opponents
- Simulate real confrontation stress

**Exercise:**
```
Setup:
- You stand in center
- 2-3 partners surround you
- Partners take turns pushing from any direction
- You respond and redirect to next partner

Duration: 2-3 minutes
Quality: Stay calm, sensitive, responsive
```

---

### Method 4: Scenario Training

**Purpose:**
- Apply techniques in realistic context
- Develop strategic thinking
- Bridge gap between drill and application

**Scenarios:**
```
1. "Cornered" Scenario:
   - You're backed against wall
   - Can't retreat, must neutralize and counter
   - Focus: Compact techniques, fa jin at close range

2. "Multiple Attackers" Scenario:
   - 2-3 partners attack sequentially
   - Must maintain awareness, move efficiently
   - Focus: Positioning, timing, quick neutralization

3. "Weapon" Scenario:
   - Partner holds foam weapon (bat, knife)
   - You must disarm or control
   - Focus: Distance, timing, controlling weapon arm
```

## Part 7: Common Questions

### Q: How often should I practice push hands?

**Ian's Answer:**
> "Minimum twice weekly for progress. Once weekly maintains what you have but doesn't develop new sensitivity. Three to four times weekly is optimal for rapid development. However, quality of practice matters more than quantity - focused, mindful practice for 30 minutes beats 2 hours of mindless shoving."

---

### Q: Can I develop fa jin without a partner?

**Ian's Answer:**
> "Yes, to a degree. Form practice with intent develops the neuromuscular pathways. Wall pushing and equipment training build isolated power. But partner training is essential for timing, sensitivity, and understanding how fa jin works against resistance. Solo training builds the engine; partner training teaches you to drive."

**Solo Training Without Partner:**
```
1. Form with fa jin at application points
2. Wall or bag issuance (100-200 strikes per session)
3. Visualization training (mental rehearsal)
4. Standing postures for structure
5. Silk reeling for whole-body connection

Limitation: Can't develop timing or sensitivity without partner
```

---

### Q: Is push hands safe? What about injuries?

**Ian's Answer:**
> "Push hands is very safe when practiced correctly. The key is control and mutual benefit. You're not trying to hurt your partner - you're helping each other learn. Competitive push hands at tournaments can be risky if ego takes over. Training push hands should be cooperative with occasional testing."

**Safety Guidelines:**
- Start at 30% intensity, build gradually
- No joint locks unless trained and agreed upon
- No strikes to head or groin in training
- Maintain peng structure to protect yourself
- Communicate with partner about intensity
- Warm up adequately before practice

**Common Minor Issues:**
- Sore shoulders (too much arm tension) → Relax more
- Knee pain (improper alignment) → Check knee tracking
- Lower back pain (leaning too far) → Maintain upright posture

---

### Q: How do I know if my fa jin is developing correctly?

**Signs of Correct Development:**
- Sound quality: Sharp "crack" not dull "thud"
- Partner's experience: Falls backward, not pushed away
- Your sensation: Effortless, relaxed after issuance
- Equipment response: Bag swings violently, not just surface compression
- Fatigue: Can issue repeatedly without tiring quickly

**Signs of Incorrect Development:**
- Arm fatigue after 10-20 strikes
- Partner feels "push" not "strike"
- Winding up before issuing
- Holding breath during issuance
- Can't issue again immediately after first

---

### Q: Should I prioritize form practice or push hands?

**Ian's Answer:**
> "Both are essential, but sequence matters. First year: 80% form, 20% push hands. Form builds the foundation - structure, alignment, coordination. Without it, push hands just reinforces bad habits. Years 2-3: 60% form, 40% push hands. As form becomes stable, push hands tests and refines it. Years 4+: 50/50 or push hands dominant if martial focus."
