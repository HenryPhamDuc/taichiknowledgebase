# Advanced Basics

## What This Section Covers

You've finished the Foundation material. Your posture holds, your weight transfers feel natural, and the basic movements are no longer something you have to think about. Now what?

This section bridges Foundation and full Advanced practice. The movements you know are about to gain depth. You're no longer learning *what* to do — you're learning *how* to do it with finer awareness, more sensitivity, and the first real taste of partner work.

I get asked often: *"If I've already learned the form, what's left to work on?"* Plenty. The form is the doorway, not the room. The room is everything that lives inside those same shapes.

## What You'll Work On

### Intermediate Form Sequences

Beyond the 24-Form, the next step is often the 42-Form Competition Routine or a traditional Yang-style long form (88, 108 movements). These longer forms:

- Extend the time you spend in each posture, deepening internal work
- Introduce transitions you don't see in the simplified form
- Challenge your memory in a way that builds presence (you have to be *here* to remember where you are)
- Reveal weak spots — you'll quickly find the sections you've been avoiding

**A practical path:** spend one month on a 48-form or 88-form long form before chasing martial applications. The repetition is what trains the nervous system.

### Partner Work Introduction

Push hands (推手) is the first time Tai Chi becomes a conversation. You and a partner maintain contact, listen to each other's structure, and respond without resistance.

The goal isn't to "win" — it's to develop *ting jin* (listening energy). You're training your nervous system to read weight, direction, and intention through physical contact. This is also where many students discover they're holding tension they didn't know they had.

**First exercises:**

- Single-hand circle (both hands, slow rotation, no force)
- Two-hand fixed step (a gentle push-pull exchange)
- Four-square pattern (moving through cardinal directions while maintaining contact)

Start slow. If you can feel your partner's intention before they move, you're doing it right.

### Push Hands Fundamentals

The principles from push hands shape everything that follows in the Advanced level. You'll learn:

- **Adhering (粘):** contact without sticking
- **Following (隨):** moving with their movement, not against it
- **Yielding (化):** neutralizing their force without losing your center
- **Issuing (發):** returning force when they're off-balance

These four words — adhere, follow, yield, issue — are the working vocabulary of internal martial arts. Master them, and you have a foundation for the rest of your life.

### Energy Sensitivity Drills

Before you can use internal energy, you have to feel it. These drills develop the sensitivity to notice what's happening inside your own body and to read what your partner is doing without looking.

- **Standing meditation with partner** — two people in Wuji stance, hands an inch apart, breathing together
- **Single-pole exercise** — one arm extended, partner applies gentle pressure in different directions, you maintain your structure
- **Listening hands** — eyes closed, partner touches your wrist lightly, you feel which direction their intention is moving

These drills look simple. They are not. Expect to spend months on each one before the sensations become clear.

## Prerequisites

Before you start this section, make sure you can honestly say yes to these:

- All Foundation basics feel natural, not effortful
- You can demonstrate the 24-Form from memory without thinking about what's next
- You practice at least 15 minutes most days — consistency matters more than duration
- You understand the 10 Essentials intellectually and can name at least three of them in your own body
- You're not in a rush — Advanced is a long road, not a destination

## How to Approach This Material

**Find a partner if you can.** Push hands and sensitivity work require another body. Solo practice is necessary, but it's not sufficient. If you don't have a local partner, look for an online community, attend a workshop, or travel to a teacher a few times a year. Solo practice builds foundation; partner practice reveals what's missing.

**Slow down to speed up.** The temptation at this level is to add more — more forms, more techniques, more exercises. Resist it. Master one drill at the depth where it becomes invisible, then move to the next.

**Keep a practice journal.** Note what you felt, what worked, what surprised you. Internal development is invisible to the eye, so you need a record. Six months from now, you'll be grateful you wrote things down.

**Return to Foundation monthly.** The Advanced material exposes every weakness in your basics. That's a feature, not a bug. When you find a weakness, go back to the relevant Foundation exercise and rebuild it.
